*Hosts: **ALEX** and **MAYA***

**MAYA:** Welcome back. Today's three genera share a family, *Morganellaceae*, and one enzyme, **phenylalanine deaminase**.

**ALEX:** That's the unifying idea. *Proteus*, *Providencia* and *Morganella* form a group defined by its ability to **degrade amino acids oxidatively**. Having phenylalanine deaminase (PAD) is characteristic and **clearly significant in preliminary identification**. If MILF shows PAD-positive, you're in this group.

## PART A — *PROTEUS*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** The genus has **8 species**, and only **3 are medically important**: ***P. vulgaris*, *P. mirabilis* and *P. penneri***. Others include *P. myxofaciens* and *P. hauseri*.

**MAYA:** And two former members moved out.

**ALEX:** ***P. morganii* became *Morganella morganii***, and ***P. rettgeri* became *Providencia rettgeri***. Today they all sit in the family ***Morganellaceae*** within the order *Enterobacterales*.

### Habitat

**ALEX:** It's ubiquitous: **putrefying organic matter**, food, soil and water. It's part of the human intestinal flora, and *P. vulgaris* is routinely isolated from stool as part of the normal microbiota.

### General characters

**ALEX:**
- Gram-negative rods with marked **polymorphism**. The name comes from ancient mythology, after **Proteus, the shape-shifting sea god**. On a smear you can see rods, coccobacilli and filaments of **30–80 µm**.
- **Very motile**, with many peritrichous flagella (non-flagellated strains also exist). Non-capsulated, non-sporulating.
- Metabolically:
  - ferment glucose **with gas**;
  - produce **H₂S**, **urease** and **phenylalanine deaminase**;
  - **don't ferment lactose**;
  - **don't produce lysine decarboxylase or β-galactosidase**.
- Aerobic, facultatively anaerobic, tolerant of large swings in temperature and pH.
- In liquid peptone media they give off a characteristic **putrefaction odor**.

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:**
- **O antigen** (LPS, endotoxin), group-specific. **H antigen** (flagellar, protein), shared by several types.
- An antigenic scheme by **Kauffmann and Perch**, modeled on the *Salmonella* Kauffmann-White scheme, with **55 O factors and 31 H factors**. Serovars proved to be good epidemiological markers, especially in **urology units** where cross-infection is common. Serotyping was advocated as early as **1917 by Weil and Felix**.
- Strains **OX-19, OX-2 and OX-K** (Vinkle scheme) share antigens with **rickettsiae**. They're used as antigens in the **Weil-Felix agglutination reaction**, for serodiagnosis of primary rickettsial infections.

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:**
- Pathogenic through **multiplication and invasiveness**, under special conditions: leaving its natural habitat, or a host with immune deficiencies.
- **Endotoxin**, released when the cell wall is destroyed.
- **Exceptional motility.** It allows **ascending invasion of the urinary tract** and **"swarming" over catheter surfaces**.
- **Urease.** It **alkalinizes urine** and promotes **stone formation, struvite stones**. Stones then promote further UTIs or make them chronic, and crystals plus biofilm protect bacteria from antibiotics.
- **Remarkable resistance to antibiotics and chemotherapeutics.** It's also relatively resistant in the environment, in food, in antiseptic solutions and in detergents, which is a hospital problem. It **survives for months at low temperatures, including in frozen food**.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:**
- Mainly **outside the digestive tract**. ***P. mirabilis* is, after *E. coli*, the most frequent cause of urinary infections.** *P. vulgaris* has also been implicated in **gastroenteritis resembling food poisoning**.
- Accidentally: localized infections, especially **abdominal surgical wounds**, genital infections, pleurisy, otitis, septicemia, metastatic sites during bacteremia, and nosocomial infections.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1. Collection.**
- **Urine** (most often), pus, purulent exudates, feces, vomit, food, blood.
- Urine: transport **within 30 minutes, otherwise "cold" at +4 °C**. Macroscopically it may be cloudy or purulent.

**2. Macro and micro.**
- Macroscopically: cloudy or purulent urine.
- Microscopically: a wet mount of the centrifuged urine sediment shows **large numbers of very motile bacteria**, leukocytes and red cells. A stained smear from homogenized urine can also be made: Gram-negative rods with marked polymorphism (rods, coccobacilli, filaments up to 30 µm), non-capsulated, non-sporulating.
- Same thresholds as for *E. coli*: **≥1 leukocyte per oil field** suggests pyuria, and **1–2 bacteria per field** with a 0.01 ml calibrated loop suggests **≥10⁵ CFU/ml**.

**3. Culture.**
- It grows on simple media, including liquid peptone media, with a putrid smell.
- **Quantitative urine culture is mandatory** (≥10⁵ CFU/ml).

**MAYA:** And the problem on plain plates.

**ALEX:** On simple media, blood agar and AABTL **you can't get isolated colonies, because of swarming (invasion or migration)**. That spreading growth itself suggests *Proteus*.

**MAYA:** How do you stop it?

**ALEX:** Swarming is **inhibited by bile salts, sodium thiosulfate, bismuth sulfite, sulfonamides, neomycin, activated charcoal and acids**. In other words, it's inhibited on the **selective-differential media** (MacConkey, ADCL), where you get **isolated, lactose-negative S colonies**. And there's a catch: on selective media, *Proteus* behaves as a lactose-negative group, and **its colonies can't be distinguished from *Salmonella* or *Shigella***.

**4a. Morphology.** Gram-negative rods, highly polymorphic (rods, coccobacilli, filaments of 30–80 µm), very motile, non-capsulated, non-sporulating.

**4b. Culture: three characteristic phenomena with identification value.**

**ALEX:** This is a favorite exam topic.

1. **Swarming (invasion or migration).** Inoculate the edge of a plate of **simple 2% agar with no inhibitors**. *Proteus* grows in **successive concentric waves**, covering the whole surface with a continuous sheet of growth. It's characteristic of the genus.

2. **The "demarcation line" phenomenon (Dienes phenomenon).** Inoculate **two different *Proteus* strains** at opposite points of the same plate. The waves **stop 1–2 mm apart**, leaving a **demarcation line** between them. If the strains are **identical**, the waves **merge** into one continuous sheet. It's used as an **epidemic marker**.

3. **The "climbing" phenomenon.** Inoculate the specimen into the **condensation water of a slant tube** and incubate it **upright**. In 24 hours you get a **pure culture of *Proteus***, grown in **stacked waves** up to the top of the medium. It's a **method of isolation in pure culture**.

Plus, on selective media: isolated lactose-negative S colonies.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- TSI: glucose + (sometimes with gas), lactose –, **H₂S +**.
- MIU/MILF: motile +, **urease +**, **PAD +** (the key character for the group), LDC –.
- Simmons citrate variable (d). β-galactosidase –.

**MAYA:** What separates *Proteus* from the other lactose-negatives?

**ALEX:** **PAD production plus absence of lysine decarboxylase.** And **indole production by *P. vulgaris* separates the *Proteus* species at the preliminary stage.**

**MAYA:** Species differentiation. There's a correction in the document.

**ALEX:** Yes, it matters. There are four tests, **indole, ornithine decarboxylase, Simmons citrate and maltose**, using Farmer 1995 percentages (Buiuc 31-24):
- ***P. mirabilis***: **indole 2, ODC 99, citrate 65, maltose 0**;
- ***P. vulgaris***: **indole 98, ODC 0, citrate 15, maltose 97**;
- ***P. penneri***: indole 0, ODC 0, citrate 0, maltose 100.

**The document warns that in the scanned copy of Table 31-24 the two rows appear swapped.** The values above are given in the correct order, consistent with the classic rule: ***P. mirabilis* is indole-negative, ODC-positive and maltose-negative. *P. vulgaris* is indole-positive, ODC-negative and maltose-positive.**

**MAYA:** Good to know if your textbook copy looks "wrong."

**ALEX:** Also, ***P. myxofaciens* strains don't produce H₂S**, and *P. penneri* produces it in only 30% of strains.

**MAYA:** Then the three-genus table, 31-26.

**ALEX:**
| | Indole | Urease | Citrate | ODC | D-xylose | Swarming on 2% agar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ***Proteus*** | v | + | d | v | + | **+** |
| ***Providencia*** | + | v | **+** | – | – | – |
| ***Morganella*** | + | + | **–** | **+** | – | – |

**ALEX:** Definitive identification with an extended set is used only in rare cases of phenotypic atypia.

**4d. Antigens.** Known anti-O and anti-H sera, by agglutination, at the **reference-lab** level (Kauffmann-Perch scheme: 55 O, 31 H).
**4e.** Not routine.
**4f.** PCR for urease genes and resistance determinants.
**4g. Phage typing.** Proposed by **Farmer**, and in Romania by **Popovici et al.** Its yield is **lower than other epidemiological markers**.
**4h. Other typing.**
- **Resistotyping** can use β-lactams, aminoglycosides and floxacins, to which *Proteus* strains show varied degrees and spectra of susceptibility.
- **The Dienes demarcation line**, a simple and accessible epidemic marker.

**4i.** MALDI-TOF; PFGE/WGS for nosocomial urology outbreaks.

**5. Susceptibility.**
- **Mandatory**, usually disk diffusion; MIC and MBC may be needed.
- **Natural resistance in all *Proteus* species: tetracyclines, tigecycline, colistin/polymyxins, nitrofurantoin.** The practical consequence: ***Proteus* UTIs are not treated with nitrofurantoin.**
- ***P. vulgaris* and *P. penneri*** are additionally naturally resistant to **aminopenicillins and cefuroxime** (class A chromosomal β-lactamase, "K1"/CumA).
- ***P. mirabilis***, by contrast, is **naturally susceptible to ampicillin and cephalosporins**. It often acquires ESBLs (CTX-M-2, VEB) and plasmid AmpC.

### Serology

**ALEX:** **Not done to diagnose *Proteus* infection.** The post-infection humoral response has no practical value. **The Weil-Felix reaction** (agglutination with OX-19, OX-2, OX-K) is a **cross-reactive serological test**, historically used to diagnose **rickettsial diseases**, not *Proteus* infection.

### Treatment

**ALEX:** **Always according to the antibiogram.** In **struvite lithiasis**, the obstruction must be removed and the stones taken out, **otherwise the infection recurs**.

## PART B — *MORGANELLA*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** At **Brenner**'s proposal, *Proteus morganii* was reconsidered as a **separate genus**, based on DNA hybridization. **The only species is *M. morganii***. Today two subspecies are described: *M. morganii* subsp. *morganii* and subsp. *sibonii*. Family *Morganellaceae*.

### Habitat

**ALEX:** **Poorly studied.** In humans it's been isolated from feces.

### General characters

**ALEX:** Motile enterobacteria that produce **PAD, urease, ornithine decarboxylase and indole**. They **don't produce β-galactosidase or H₂S** and **don't grow on Simmons citrate**.

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:** A scheme with **34 O and 25 H factors**, with limited use.

### Virulence / clinical significance

**ALEX:** Conditionally pathogenic; endotoxin; urease (urine alkalinization). It's known as a cause of **postoperative urinary infections**, and more rarely generalized infections.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1.** Urine, wounds, blood.
**2.** Nonspecific; motile Gram-negative rods.
**3. Culture.**
- Grows easily on simple agar and on weakly or moderately selective media, giving **lactose-negative S colonies**.
- **Unlike *Proteus*, it does NOT swarm.** That's the key distinguishing feature.

**4a.** Motile, non-sporulating, non-capsulated Gram-negative rods.
**4b.** Lactose-negative S colonies, no swarming.
**4c. Biochemistry.**
- TSI/MILF: **small amounts of gas** from glucose, no lactose or sucrose fermentation, **H₂S-negative**. On MILF: motile, **indole +**, **PAD +**, LDC –.
- **Citrate –**, **urease +**, **ODC +**, β-galactosidase –.
- That combination can be confused with other members of the *Proteus/Providencia* group. It's separated by the restricted set in Table 31-26: **citrate-negative + ODC-positive + xylose-negative + no swarming**.
- Definitive identification uses the extended biochemical set (Table 31-5).

**4d.** Serotyping (34 O, 25 H), limited.
**4e.** Not done.
**4f.** PCR for resistance determinants (chromosomal **DHA-like AmpC**).
**4g.** No widely used phage typing schemes of its own.
**4h. Other typing.** **Resistotyping** applies under the same conditions as for *Proteus*. Buiuc notes that ***M. morganii* is often susceptible to nitrofurantoin and tetracycline**, which can serve as intraspecific or intergeneric markers. The document says to **correlate this with the current natural-resistance data** below, which contradict it.

**MAYA:** So that's an old textbook statement versus EUCAST.

**ALEX:** Exactly. Know both, and know that current expected phenotypes list *Morganella* as naturally resistant to both.

**4i.** MALDI-TOF; WGS for outbreaks.

**5. Susceptibility.**
- **Natural resistance**: aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, **first- and second-generation cephalosporins**, cefoxitin (inducible AmpC), **colistin/polymyxins, tetracyclines, tigecycline, nitrofurantoin**.
- Naturally susceptible to third-generation cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides and carbapenems, **but with a risk of AmpC derepression during treatment**.

### Serology / Treatment

**ALEX:** No serology. Treat according to the antibiogram. **Avoid aminopenicillins, first- and second-generation cephalosporins, colistin and nitrofurantoin.**

## PART C — *PROVIDENCIA*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** Recently restructured, the genus has **6 species**, **4 of them isolated from human specimens**: ***P. rettgeri*** (formerly *Proteus rettgeri*), ***P. stuartii***, ***P. alcalifaciens*** and ***P. rustigianii***. Family *Morganellaceae*.

### Habitat

**ALEX:** Environment and digestive tract. A **nosocomial reservoir**: urinary catheters and **long-stay units**.

### General characters

**ALEX:**
- Metabolically similar to *Proteus*: **PAD-positive**; lactose-, β-galactosidase- and LDC-negative.
- It differs from *Proteus* by **not swarming on 2% agar**, by **splitting malonate**, and by **growing on Simmons citrate**.
- **Except *P. alcalifaciens*, the species produce no gas or very little from glucose.**

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:** Schemes based on O and H antigens:
- *P. rettgeri*: **34 O and 26 H factors**, defining **45 serovars**;
- *P. stuartii* and *P. alcalifaciens* together: **62 O and 30 H factors**, defining **45 serovars**.

### Virulence / clinical significance

**ALEX:** Conditionally pathogenic; endotoxin; urease (variable); adherence to catheters.
- ***P. rettgeri* and *P. stuartii*** cause urinary and extraintestinal infections, **mainly nosocomial**.
- ***P. alcalifaciens***, rarely isolated from humans, has been implicated in **food-poisoning outbreaks** or sporadic acute diarrhea.
- *P. rustigianii* is isolated sporadically and insignificantly from humans.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1.** Urine (**chronically catheterized patients**), wounds, blood, feces (outbreak investigations).
**2.** Nonspecific.
**3.** Culture places them among the **lactose-negative** enterobacteria, with S colonies and **no swarming**.
**4a.** Motile Gram-negative rods.
**4b.** Lactose-negative S colonies, no migration on 2% agar.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- Enough for genus assignment on TSI + MILF: **little or no gas** from glucose, **H₂S-negative**, **PAD and indole positive**, in a culture that **does NOT swarm**, plus **Simmons citrate positive, malonate split, ODC negative, xylose negative**.
- Species differentiation (Buiuc 31-25, % positive):
  - ***P. rettgeri***: gas 10, **mannitol 100**, **adonitol 100**, inositol 90, rhamnose 70, trehalose 0, **urease 98**;
  - ***P. stuartii***: gas 0, mannitol 10, adonitol 5, inositol 95, rhamnose 0, **trehalose 98**, urease 30;
  - ***P. alcalifaciens***: **gas 85**, mannitol 2, adonitol 98, inositol 1, urease 0;
  - ***P. rustigianii***: gas 35, the rest negative.
- Definitive identification uses extended sets (conventional or microtest).

**4d.** Serological identification by the O/H schemes above, in specialized labs.
**4e.** Not done.
**4f.** PCR for resistance genes.
**4g.** No widely used phage typing schemes.
**4h. Other typing.**
- ***P. rettgeri* is phenotypically and genotypically heterogeneous.** It contains **4 biovariants** defined by the ability to ferment **rhamnose and salicin**. Built into a biotyping scheme, these two tests can work as **epidemiological markers**.
- **Resistotyping** is easy, using β-lactams, aminoglycosides and fluoroquinolones, which are generally active on *Proteus/Providencia*.
**4i.** MALDI-TOF; WGS for outbreaks. ***P. stuartii* is a typical colonizer of urinary catheters in long-term care facilities.**

**5. Susceptibility.**
- **Natural resistance**: aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, first-generation cephalosporins, cefoxitin, **colistin/polymyxins, tetracyclines, tigecycline, nitrofurantoin**.
- ***P. stuartii* and *P. rettgeri* are also naturally resistant to gentamicin and tobramycin**, through the acetyltransferase **AAC(2′)-Ia** in *P. stuartii*. **Amikacin usually remains active.**

### Serology / Treatment

**ALEX:** No serology. Treat according to the antibiogram.
