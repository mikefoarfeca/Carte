## PART A — *VIBRIO*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** *Vibrio* used to belong to the family ***Vibrionaceae*** (1965), together with *Aeromonas*, *Photobacterium* and *Plesiomonas*. Recent molecular and chemotaxonomic studies **dispute that this family is real**. Still, their habitat and their phenotypic and pathogenic similarities justify **treating the three genera together for identification** in clinical microbiology.

**MAYA:** The hybridization data.

**ALEX:** rRNA–DNA hybridization shows **at least 80% relatedness between *Vibrio* and *Photobacterium***, but **under 60% with *Aeromonas* and *Plesiomonas***. That justifies separating the taxa.

**MAYA:** How many species?

**ALEX:** **Over 48 species** (36 according to Buiuc), and **at least 12** are pathogenic or potentially pathogenic for humans:
- ***V. cholerae* O:1 and O:139**, the **cholera vibrios**;
- *V. cholerae* non-O:1;
- *V. mimicus*;
- ***V. parahaemolyticus***;
- ***V. vulnificus***;
- *V. alginolyticus, V. fluvialis, V. furnissii, V. hollisae, V. damsela, V. cincinnatiensis, V. metschnikovii, V. carchariae*;
- *Photobacterium damselae*.

**ALEX:** The genus is phylogenetically heterogeneous: there's the *V. cholerae–V. mimicus* group, the *V. fluvialis–V. furnissii* complex, and the *V. parahaemolyticus–V. alginolyticus* line. *V. anguillarum* and *V. damsela* have been proposed for reclassification into *Listonella* and *Photobacterium*, respectively.

### Habitat

**ALEX:**
- **Aquatic organisms**: marine, estuarine, coastal, lagoon and fresh waters. They're abundant in marine and estuarine waters, living in symbiosis with the vertebrates and invertebrates there.
- **Halophilic species** (*V. parahaemolyticus, V. vulnificus*, etc.) are **limited to sea water**. **Non-halophilic species** (*V. cholerae, V. mimicus*) also spread into **fresh water** (rivers, lakes) far from the sea, where they're present only transiently.
- ***V. cholerae*** is found in the gut and feces of cholera patients. Its presence in the environment is explained by fecal contamination and survival, and there may also be an **extraintestinal life cycle**.

**MAYA:** And the three forms of persistence in sea water.

**ALEX:**
1. **The free form**, as long as the water provides nutrients.
2. **The epibiotic form**: close association with the **chitin matrix of zooplankton, shellfish and copepods**, helped by **chitinases** and amino-sugar binding proteins. **Epibiotic vibrios persist longer and get past the gastric acid barrier more effectively.**
3. **The "dormant" form**: **microvibrios and viable but non-culturable (VBNC)** cells, demonstrated with immunofluorescent stains.

### General characters

**ALEX:**
- **Straight or comma-curved** Gram-negative rods. The comma shape has become "more of a historical image." They're **1.4–2.6 µm long and 0.5–0.8 µm wide**, non-sporulating, non-capsulated and **very motile**. In liquid media they move with **polar flagella**, single or multiple. **On solid media they use lateral flagella** with a shorter wavelength than the polar flagellum.
- **Catalase- and oxidase-positive.** The **exception is *V. metschnikovii*, which is oxidase-negative** because it lacks cytochrome c.
- Undemanding, facultatively anaerobic. They attack sugars **fermentatively, without gas**, and have both respiratory and fermentative metabolism.
- **Sensitive to the vibriostatic agent O/129** (2,4-diamino-6,7-diisopropylpteridine) at **10 and/or 150 µg**. This is a classic genus character, but **its value has become relative: over 90% of *V. cholerae* O:1 strains isolated in India are resistant to both concentrations.**
- They produce extracellular enzymes: cytotoxic and cytolytic toxins, neuraminidase, chitinase, protease, lipase.
- They're adapted to varied salinities. **NaCl stimulates growth in some species and is mandatory for others (halophiles).** ***V. cholerae* grows well at alkaline pH (9–9.5)**, which is the basis of the enrichment media.
- **Survival**: very sensitive to sunlight and heat (killed almost instantly at 100 °C) and to antiseptics. They live **up to 7 days in food**, a few days in water depending on sewage treatment, and **several weeks in the intestine of untreated patients**.

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:**
- **O antigen** (LPS) in the cell wall. By the structure of the somatic O group antigen, cholera vibrios were split into **6 serogroups**. **The classical cholera vibrio and the El Tor biotype both belong to group O:1.** The others are **non-O:1, "NAG" (non-agglutinable** with anti-O:1 sera), which can't be told apart biochemically from O:1. Taxonomic work has shown that ***V. cholerae* O:1 and non-O:1 are in fact the same species**.
- **Serotypes within O:1**, defined by antigenic factors **a, b, c**:
  - **Ogawa (a, b)**;
  - **Inaba (a, c)**;
  - **Hikojima (a, b, c)**.
  The first two are commonly isolated and spread epidemically. Hikojima is very rare and not recognized by some specialists.
- **Serogroup O:139 ("Bengal")** is an **O mutant of the El Tor biovar**.
- **H antigen** (flagellar), since vibrios are motile. Antigens on the pili.
- **Cholera enterotoxin**: heat-labile, MW **84,000 Da**, made of **one A subunit (active) and 5 B subunits (binding)**.

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:**
- **The family's pathogenic mechanism**: adherence to the epithelium (adhesins, pili), then colonization, invasion of the mucosa, and production of biologically active substances: extracellular enzymes, enterotoxins, cytotoxins and hemolysins.
- **Chemotactic capacity** toward mucosal epithelium is more evident in **monotrichous forms** and depends on the amount and quality of mucus.
- **Adhesins**: surface protein components linked to fimbriae and pili, which bind stereochemically to complementary tissue molecules. **The colonization factors are made in parallel with the enterotoxin and controlled by the same gene. The pili are lost once infection is established.**
- **The S layer** outside the cell wall, seen by electron microscopy in virulent strains, protects against lytic agents (bacteriophages, serum proteins).

**MAYA:** Now the toxin mechanism. Step by step.

**ALEX:** **Cholera enterotoxin is the main cause of secretory diarrhea.**
1. **The B subunits have high affinity for GM1 ganglioside receptors** on target cells.
2. After binding, **the A subunit enters the cytoplasm**.
3. It catalyzes **transfer of ADP-ribose from NAD**, activating the **membrane regulatory protein Gs**.
4. Activated Gs drives **increased cAMP** in the small intestinal mucosal cells.
5. This causes **hypersecretion of chloride and bicarbonate**.
6. A **massive efflux of water** follows.

**ALEX:** And the experiment: **volunteers who swallowed less than 10 µg of purified toxin developed "explosive" diarrhea of over 20 liters within 2 days.**

**MAYA:** Wow. Other toxins?

**ALEX:**
- **Thermostable direct hemolysin (TDH)** is responsible for the **Kanagawa phenomenon**. It's a **major pathogenicity factor of *V. parahaemolyticus***, together with a related cytotoxin, and it's been found in other species too.
- **The capsule** is an important virulence factor in ***V. vulnificus***.
- **Endotoxin** is released from the wall when the bacterium is destroyed.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:** **Cholera** is a gastroenteritis of major public-health importance, caused by **serogroups O:1 and O:139 of *V. cholerae***.
- **The infective dose is high: 10⁸–10¹⁰ organisms.**
- **It isn't an invasive disease.** The bacteria **don't enter the bloodstream**. They stay in the intestinal tract, colonize the **brush border** of epithelial cells, multiply and release toxin.
- **Incubation 1–4 days.** **Sudden onset** with nausea, vomiting, **profuse diarrhea (20–30 L/day)** and abdominal cramps.
- **Watery, "rice-water" stools**, with mucus, epithelial cells and **enormous numbers of vibrios**.
- Rapid fluid and electrolyte loss leads to **dehydration, circulatory collapse and anuria**. **Untreated mortality is 25–50%.**

**MAYA:** And the other species, Table 31-28.

**ALEX:**
- **Gastroenteritis**: *V. cholerae* O:1/O:139 (+++), *V. cholerae* non-O:1 (+++), *V. mimicus*, *V. fluvialis*, ***V. parahaemolyticus* (+++, after eating seafood)**, *V. hollisae*.
- **Wound infections**: *V. cholerae* non-O:1, *V. alginolyticus*, *V. vulnificus*, *V. carchariae*. These occur in **people rescued from drowning, wounds contaminated with sea water, and shark bites**.
- **Septicemia**: ***V. vulnificus* (+++)**, with risk factors of **liver disease, age over 40, and male sex**.
- **Pneumonia**: *V. vulnificus*, *V. alginolyticus*.
- **Eye and ear infections**: *V. alginolyticus*, *V. cholerae* non-O:1.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

#### 1. Collection and transport

**ALEX:**
- **It's an epidemiological emergency.** The diagnosis has to be made as fast as possible.
- Specimen: **feces** (most often) and vomit. Collect quickly, before antibiotics.
- Macroscopically: **watery, rice-water stools, greenish, foul-smelling, with mucus flakes**.
- **Transport within 1–3 hours at most.** Transport medium: **Cary-Blair**. **Alkaline peptone water (pH 9–9.5) can serve both as transport medium and as enrichment medium.**
- From wounds, in a suggestive setting (sea-water-contaminated wounds, shark bites), isolation can be done on a **non-selective medium**.

#### 2. Macro and micro

**ALEX:**
- Macroscopically: the **rice-water** look of the stool.
- Microscopically: **a fresh wet mount between slide and coverslip, NO smears**, examined at **40×**, **preferably on dark field**.

**MAYA:** And the key finding.

**ALEX:** **Essentially, NO leukocytes are seen, because cholera isn't an invasive disease.** Vibrios are present in large numbers with **active "tumbling" movements** or chaotic darting typical of monoflagellate organisms.
- **The immobilization test**: treat the specimen with **specific antibodies**. **If motility stops, the diagnosis is confirmed.**
- Grown under optimal conditions they're Gram-negative rods of 0.5–0.8 by 1.4–2.6 µm. **In young cultures curved forms predominate**, occasionally S-shaped. **As the culture ages, straight forms increase** and they become hard to distinguish from other enteric rods. **On media with suboptimal NaCl, vibrios look pleomorphic** (especially halophiles).

#### 3. Culture

**ALEX:**
- Undemanding. They grow on simple media, including **alkaline peptone water (APW)**, where they form a **surface "veil."** That's useful for primary isolation.
- **Selectivity relies on the vibrios' ability to grow at pH 8.6**, which is unfavorable to many bacteria, and to **tolerate toxic concentrations of organic compounds** (bile, bile salts) **or inorganic ones** (potassium iodide, potassium tellurite).
- Two media are used in parallel: a **moderately selective BSA (Bile Salts Agar)** and a **highly selective TCBS (Thiosulphate-Citrate-Bile salts-Sucrose)**. Selectivity varies by lot and manufacturer.

**MAYA:** The recommended scheme.

**ALEX:** **Inoculate the specimen into APW**, directly or after transport in Cary-Blair. **After 6–8 hours at 35–37 °C, plate onto the selective medium, taking 2 loopfuls from the surface of the APW**, where the veil forms.

**MAYA:** What do the colonies look like?

**ALEX:**
- **On TCBS:**
  - ***V. cholerae*: yellow colonies (sucrose fermentation), large, 2–4 mm;**
  - *V. parahaemolyticus, V. mimicus, V. hollisae*: **green** colonies;
  - *V. vulnificus*: green (85%) or yellow (15%);
  - *V. hollisae, V. damsela* and *V. cincinnatiensis* grow poorly or not at all on TCBS.
  - **Careful**: *Proteus* can grow on TCBS too, and *Aeromonas* and *Plesiomonas* variably.
- **On BSA**: S colonies, round, shiny and **transparent like dewdrops**, unlike *E. coli* colonies, which are matt.
- **On non-selective media with 0.5–1% NaCl**: colonies of 2–5 mm, flat or slightly convex, smooth, moist and shiny. Variants can be transparent or opaque. **Rough variants make wrinkled colonies that are hard to emulsify.**
- **Only some *Vibrio* species grow on MacConkey** (e.g. *V. cholerae*), forming **lactose-negative** colonies.
- **On 5% sheep, rabbit or horse blood agar**: *V. damsela, V. fluvialis, V. hollisae, V. metschnikovii* and, usually, **non-O:1 *V. cholerae*** strains produce **β-hemolysis** after 24 hours. **El Tor biovar strains are often β-hemolytic too.** *V. alginolyticus, V. parahaemolyticus* and *V. vulnificus* are non-hemolytic or show only a circumscribed greening.

**MAYA:** The Kanagawa phenomenon.

**ALEX:** **The Kanagawa phenomenon is β-hemolysis by *V. parahaemolyticus* on Wagatsuma agar.** Wagatsuma agar has **D-mannitol as carbon source, 7% NaCl, and human group O or rabbit blood**.

- Without optimal salt, most halophilic species don't grow. In broth they form a pellicle and uniform turbidity.

#### 4. Identification

**4a. Morphology.** Gram-negative rods, 1–3 by 0.5–0.8 µm, curved (comma) or straight, very motile, **polar monotrichous**, non-sporulating, non-capsulated.

**4b. Culture.** On TCBS, large yellow colonies of 2–4 mm (*V. cholerae*). On BSA, transparent S colonies "like dewdrops." In APW, a surface veil.

**4c. Biochemistry.**

**MAYA:** Preliminary tests first.

**ALEX:** There are two imperatives: **culture halophiles on media with optimal NaCl**, and **identify *V. cholerae* urgently**. The second is helped by the species being non-halophilic.

- **The oxidase test** is positive in vibrios. It separates *Vibrio*, *Aeromonas* and *Plesiomonas shigelloides* (oxidase +) from **enterobacteria** (oxidase –), such as ***Proteus vulgaris*, which also grows on TCBS**. Only *V. metschnikovii* is oxidase-negative.
  - **Careful: oxidase done on TCBS isolates can be falsely positive because of the sucrose, so do it on a non-selective medium.**
- **The O/129 vibriostatic test**: disks of **10 µg and 150 µg** on trypticase soy agar, inoculated as for disk-diffusion AST with a **0.5 McFarland** suspension. **Any zone of inhibition after overnight incubation means a positive test.**
- **Tolerance to 6–6.5% NaCl** correctly separates *Vibrio* (tolerant) from *Aeromonas* and *Plesiomonas*, which don't grow at that salinity.
- **The "string test" for DNA**: suspend a suspect colony in a drop of **0.5% sodium deoxycholate** on a slide. **Vibrios lyse and release their DNA, which can be "pulled" with a loop like a string.** This quickly separates *Vibrio* from *Aeromonas* (both oxidase-positive with similar colonies).
- **TSI screening**: medically relevant vibrios **ferment glucose without gas**, may or may not ferment sucrose, usually (diarrheagenic species) **don't ferment lactose and don't produce H₂S**. **Diarrheagenic aeromonads produce gas from glucose. *Proteus vulgaris* produces H₂S.**

**4d. Antigens.**
- **Slide agglutination with anti-cholera O:1 serum** on isolates with **yellow colonies on TCBS and a positive oxidase on non-selective medium**. Those that agglutinate are confirmed biochemically.
- **At the reference centre** the positive reaction is confirmed and the **serotype (Ogawa, Inaba, Hikojima)** identified. **If O:1 is negative, slide agglutination with anti-O:139 serum** is done.
- **Combined biovar + serovar** gives a rough epidemiological marker.

**4e.** Pathogenicity tests not routine; enterotoxigenicity and cytotoxicity can be evaluated experimentally.

**4f. Virulence.**
- **Toxigenicity of *V. cholerae* by VET-RPLA** (reversed passive latex agglutination).
- **ELISA** for characteristic *V. cholerae* structures.
- *Modern layer*: PCR for ***ctxA/ctxB*** (cholera toxin), ***tcpA*** (toxin-coregulated pilus), ***toxR***, ***ompW***, and for ***tdh/trh*** in *V. parahaemolyticus*.

**4g. Phage typing.**
- Reserved for **reference labs**. Some systems define **over 140 phage types**.
- **El Tor biovar phage typing** was greatly improved by **adding 5 new phages to the Basu-Mukerjee set of 5 lytic phages (I–V)**, and by redefining the test dilution from confluent lysis to **near-confluent lysis**. That defined **146 lysovars, covering typing of about 99% of strains**. Discriminatory power is excellent, except for **lysovar 115 (prevalence 12%)**.

**4h. Other typing.**
- **Serotyping (Ogawa/Inaba/Hikojima) and biotyping (classical/El Tor)** are the basic epidemiological markers.
- **Molecular methods** are currently harder to access, but they give reliable markers for El Tor.

**4i. New-generation methods.**
- **Ribotyping and PCR** at reference centres (mentioned in the course).
- *Modern layer*: **MALDI-TOF**; **WGS** to track the **pandemic lineages (7PET)** and the **SXT resistance islands**; **rapid immunochromatographic dipstick tests for O1/O139 antigen**, used as **field screening in epidemics (WHO)**.

#### 5. Susceptibility testing

**ALEX:**
- **Standardized disk diffusion**, especially to **monitor the emergence of resistant strains**.
- *Modern layer*: **EUCAST has specific criteria for *V. cholerae***. Test tetracycline/doxycycline, azithromycin, ciprofloxacin and cotrimoxazole.
- **Tetracycline resistance is plasmid-controlled** (mentioned in the course too). **The integrative conjugative element SXT carries resistance to cotrimoxazole, streptomycin and chloramphenicol.**

### Serology

**ALEX:**
- **Not used in routine diagnosis.** After cholera there's immunity to reinfection, but its duration and degree are unknown.
- **Gastric acidity gives some protection** when a small number of vibrios is swallowed.
- In experimental animals **specific IgA antibodies appear in the gut lumen**. **Antibodies against the O antigen protect lab animals**, and similar antibodies appear in human serum after infection, somewhat associated with protection against colonization.
- **The presence of antitoxin antibodies is NOT associated with protection.**

### Treatment and prevention

**ALEX:**
- **The essential role belongs to restoring fluid and electrolyte balance**: rehydration with electrolyte solutions, **first parenteral**, then **oral once the gut tolerates it**.
- **Oral tetracycline reduces the diarrhea and shortens vibrio excretion.** In some endemic areas (plasmid) resistance has appeared. Alternatives: **aminoglycosides or chloramphenicol**, until the antibiogram is back.

**MAYA:** Epidemiology.

**ALEX:**
- Transmission is through **food and water contaminated with feces**.
- In endemic areas **asymptomatic carriers** play an important role, **but not through person-to-person contact**.
- **After floods and disasters the epidemic risk rises substantially.**
- **7 pandemics have been reported since 1817.** **All except the 7th started in the Ganges delta.**
- **Romania hasn't had cholera cases since 1995.**

## PART B — *AEROMONAS* AND *PLESIOMONAS*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:**
- ***Aeromonas***: taxonomy under revision. The classic species of the **psychrophilic group** (*A. salmonicida* and its subspecies) and the **mesophilic group** (*A. hydrophila, A. caviae, A. sobria*) are really **phenospecies**. DNA–DNA hybridization defined **12 hybridization groups (HG)**, which rank as **genomospecies**:
  - HG1 *A. hydrophila*, HG3 *A. salmonicida*, **HG4 *A. caviae***, HG5 *A. media*, HG6 *A. eucrenophila*, HG7 *A. sobria*, **HG8 *A. veronii* biovar *sobria***, HG9 *A. jandaei*, HG10 *A. veronii* biovar *veronii*, HG12 *A. schubertii*. HG2 and HG11 remained unnamed.
  - 16S rRNA sequencing added *A. allosaccharophila* and *A. trota*.
  - Sequence analysis justifies **family rank** for aeromonads, ***Aeromonadaceae***.
- ***Plesiomonas***: **one species, *P. shigelloides***.
- *Modern layer*: ***Plesiomonas shigelloides* is now classified in *Enterobacteriaceae*/order Enterobacterales.** **It's the only oxidase-positive species in that order.**

### Habitat

**ALEX:**
- ***Aeromonas***: commonly isolated from **fresh water** (rivers, lakes, wells, water tanks), where it **multiplies in the warm season**. Also found in the gut contents of fish, amphibians, reptiles and some higher vertebrates. It easily contaminates soil and farm produce. In marine biotopes it's only occasional.
- ***Plesiomonas shigelloides***: often isolated from **fresh and estuarine water with fecal pollution**. Concentrations depend on ambient temperature, high in summer and low in winter. Isolated from estuarine sediments, eels, fish, amphibians, crabs and shellfish. Although it lives in the gut of many warm-blooded animals (pigs, cats, dogs, poultry), **only under crowded, unhygienic conditions can it transiently colonize children's intestines**.

### General characters

**ALEX:**

***Aeromonas*:**
- Gram-negative rods, sometimes coccoid, **0.3–1 by 1–3.5 µm**, straight, **indistinguishable from coliforms**.
- **Motile by a single polar flagellum (monotrichous)**. In young cultures on agar they can become **peritrichous**.
- **All *A. salmonicida* subspecies are non-flagellated and non-motile.**
- Undemanding, **catalase- and oxidase-positive**, facultatively anaerobic. They attack sugars **fermentatively, with or without gas**, and **reduce nitrates to nitrites**.
- **Resistant to O/129.**
- They grow without added salt and are **inhibited at 6% NaCl**. They tolerate up to **4% NaCl, pH 5–9, and bile salts**.

***Plesiomonas shigelloides*:**
- Gram-negative rod, **0.8–1 by 3.0 µm**, indistinguishable from enterobacteria.
- **Motile by a single polar flagellum.** In wet mounts it shows **tumbling movements**, different from the oscillating motility of peritrichous enterobacteria.
- Undemanding, facultatively anaerobic, **catalase- and oxidase-positive**.
- Ferments **only a few carbohydrates, without gas**. **Reduces nitrates.**
- Grows on all routine media over **wide temperature (8–45 °C) and pH (4.5–8.5) ranges**. **Needs no added salt, tolerates 5% NaCl, but not 6%.**

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:**
- ***Aeromonas***: **O serogroups and ribotyping** are the epidemiological markers, available **only in reference labs**.
- ***Plesiomonas shigelloides*** **agglutinates well with anti-*Shigella sonnei* serum**, a possible source of confusion in preliminary testing.

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:**
- ***Aeromonas***: gastroenteritis is caused by **two enterotoxins**, a **β-hemolytic cytolysin (aerolysin)** and **a cytotoxin**. There are several aerolysins that are immunologically and genetically distinct but share a single mechanism. The aerolysins of *A. hydrophila* and *A. veronii* are the most closely related immunologically. In mice, ***A. hydrophila* and *A. sobria* seem more virulent than *A. caviae***.
- ***Plesiomonas***: pathogenicity factors poorly defined.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:**
- ***Aeromonas***: of 14 species, **at least 9, all mesophilic**, have been implicated in human infection. **Gastroenteritis.** Extra-digestive infections: **wound infections**, bacteriuria and, in the **immunocompromised host** (aplastic anemia, leukemia, sarcoma, **liver cirrhosis**), systemic infections.
  - **The pathogenicity score (Buiuc 31-33)**:
    - **1 = major human pathogen**: ***A. hydrophila, A. caviae, A. veronii* biovar *sobria***;
    - **2 = less frequent isolation, rarely proven etiological role**: *A. jandaei, A. schubertii*;
    - **3 = rare isolation, unknown clinical significance**: *A. veronii* biovar *veronii*, *A. trota*.
- ***Plesiomonas shigelloides***: occasional **acute diarrhea**, rarely isolated from blood cultures or CSF.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1.** Feces, wound specimens, urine, blood; food and water samples (investigations).
**2.** Nonspecific; wet mount from feces.

**3. Culture.**

***Aeromonas*:**
- Undemanding. It grows on the usual selective media for enterobacteria or vibrios. **Because it ferments lactose or sucrose (often both), it can easily be mistaken for non-pathogenic coliforms (and so missed) or for vibrios.**
- **Medium of choice: blood agar with cefsulodin-irgasan-novobiocin (CIN)**, sold commercially. **Ampicillin (5 µg/ml)** is another selective agent.
- **Mesophiles** grow best at 35–37 °C, forming **3–5 mm grey, slightly rough colonies overnight**.
- ***A. caviae* colonies are non-hemolytic.** Many strains of other species are **β-hemolytic on sheep blood agar** and could be **mistaken for *P. aeruginosa* on a quick look**. But they have **neither the metallic sheen nor the characteristic aroma** of the pyocyanic bacillus. Some strains show **double hemolysis** (outer incomplete zone, inner complete zone).
- ***A. salmonicida***, a psychrophilic fish pathogen, grows best at **22–25 °C**. On media with **1% tyrosine** it forms a **water-soluble brown pigment**. Among mesophiles, only some *A. media* and HG2 strains make pigment on heart infusion agar.

***Plesiomonas*:**
- **Inhibited and grows very poorly** on moderately selective media for vibrios. It **grows well on MacConkey**, forming **colonies that look like shigellae**.
- On blood agar after 24 h at 37 °C: **round, convex, grey, non-hemolytic colonies of 1–2 mm**.

**4a.** *Aeromonas*: polar monotrichous rods or coccobacilli. *Plesiomonas*: rod with a single polar flagellum, indistinguishable from enterobacteria.
**4b.** *Aeromonas*: 3–5 mm grey, slightly rough, often β-hemolytic colonies. *Plesiomonas*: 1–2 mm convex grey non-hemolytic colonies, *Shigella*-like on MacConkey.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- **The key test is a positive oxidase**, which separates them from enterobacteria in preliminary screening. *Aeromonas* strains can otherwise be confused with ***Escherichia*, *Enterobacter* or *Providencia***.
- **Versus *Vibrio***, use the minimal tests from Table 31-30: ***Aeromonas* is resistant to O/129 and ampicillin, doesn't grow at 6% NaCl, and is string-test negative.** ***Plesiomonas* is O/129-sensitive, doesn't grow at 6% NaCl, and makes no gas.**
- ***Aeromonas* species identification (Buiuc 31-33)**: indole, Moeller decarboxylases (arginine, lysine, ornithine), gas from glucose, VP, acid from L-arabinose, lactose, sucrose, m-inositol, D-mannitol, salicin, esculin hydrolysis, β-hemolysis on sheep blood, and resistance to ampicillin 10 µg. Landmarks:
  - ***A. hydrophila***: esculin +, VP +, arabinose +, hemolysis +;
  - ***A. caviae***: hemolysis –, ornithine –, VP +;
  - ***A. veronii* biovar *sobria***: esculin –, arabinose –, hemolysis +;
  - ***A. schubertii***: indole –, sucrose –, mannitol –;
  - ***A. trota***: **ampicillin-susceptible**, the exception to the genus's resistance.
- Extra tests for correct differentiation: DL-lactate and urocanic acid utilization, D-rhamnose fermentation (*A. hydrophila* / HG2–*A. salmonicida*); DL-lactate, mannose fermentation, pyrazinamidase and β-hemolysis (*A. caviae* / *A. media*). These are **reference-lab work**. **Errors for clinically significant isolates don't matter much**, given the pathogenicity difference between *A. hydrophila* and *A. caviae*. ***A. allosaccharophila* can't be correctly identified in a clinical lab.**
- ***Plesiomonas***: confirmed by the tests in Table 31-33 and separated from vibrios and aeromonads by Table 31-30. **Isolation and identification pose no problems.**

**4d.** *Aeromonas*: O serogroups, reference labs only. *Plesiomonas*: **watch for cross-agglutination with anti-*Shigella sonnei* serum.**
**4e.** Not routine.
**4f.** *Modern*: PCR for ***aerA*** (aerolysin), *hlyA*, *act*.
**4g.** No widely used phage typing schemes.
**4h.** **O serogroups**, epidemiological markers available only to reference labs.
**4i.** **Ribotyping** is retained as an epidemiological marker for *Aeromonas*. *Modern*: MALDI-TOF (with updated databases it separates the genomospecies well), *rpoD/gyrB* sequencing, WGS.

**5. Susceptibility.**
- Disk diffusion.
- ***Aeromonas***: **natural resistance to ampicillin (except *A. trota*) and amoxicillin-clavulanate**. It often has **multiple inducible chromosomal β-lactamases**: a penicillinase, an **AmpC** cephalosporinase and the **metallo-β-lactamase carbapenemase CphA**. **Avoid β-lactam monotherapy in severe infections.** Active drugs: fluoroquinolones, cotrimoxazole, aminoglycosides, third-generation cephalosporins.
- ***Plesiomonas***: natural resistance to aminopenicillins; susceptible to cotrimoxazole, fluoroquinolones and third-generation cephalosporins.

### Serology / Treatment

**ALEX:** No serology. **Gastroenteritis is usually self-limiting.** Severe forms and extraintestinal infections are treated according to the antibiogram.
