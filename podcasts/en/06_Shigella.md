# THE GRAM-NEGATIVE ROUNDS
## Episode 6 — *Shigella*: One Hundred Bacteria and a Dysentery

*Hosts: **ALEX** and **MAYA***

---

**[INTRO MUSIC]**

**MAYA:** Welcome back. Today's organism needs only about a hundred cells to make you ill: *Shigella*, the intestinal pathogen *par excellence*.

**ALEX:** It's also one of the few bacterial diarrheas you **must** treat with antibiotics. Let's go through it.

---

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** Molecular biology says *Shigella* should share a genus with *Escherichia*, the "*Escherichia-Shigella*" genus. For historical and practical reasons it's still treated separately.

**MAYA:** And the four subgroups.

**ALEX:** They're defined by antigenic structure, **subgroups A to D**:
- **A: *S. dysenteriae***, with **10–13 serotypes**. **Type 1 = *Shigella shiga***, the most pathogenic. Type 2 = *S. schmitzii*. Types 3–7 = the **Large-Sachs** group.
- **B: *S. flexneri***, with **6 serotypes** subdivided into subserotypes (1a, 1b, 2a, 2b, 3a, 3b, 4a, 4b, 5a, 5b, 6) and the **x, y variants**. Synonym: *S. paradysenteriae*.
- **C: *S. boydii***, with **15–18 serotypes**.
- **D: *S. sonnei***, with **one serotype** and **2 phases or variants, S and R**. Old names: Duval's bacillus, *Bacillus ceylonensis* A, *B. pseudodysenteriae* Kruse type E.

**MAYA:** And they're sometimes called serovars.

**ALEX:** Yes. Because anti-*Shigella* sera settle the identification where biochemical tests can't, some authors regard the *Shigella* species as "serovars."

### Habitat

**ALEX:**
- ***Shigella* is NOT part of the normal intestinal flora.** It's found in the intestine and stool of the **sick human**. The reservoir is exclusively **humans and primates**.
- Depending on conditions it can be isolated from water, food or objects, and it can be **carried by flies**.

### General characters

**ALEX:**
- Gram-negative rods, **non-motile**, **2–3 by 0.5–0.8 µm**, non-capsulated, non-sporulating, oxidase-negative, **lactose-negative**.
- Facultative anaerobes. **Glucose-positive WITHOUT gas**, **no H₂S**, **urease-negative**, **lysine decarboxylase-negative**. Some groups produce indole and β-galactosidase. They use sodium acetate only occasionally.
- In practice they're **very hard to distinguish from inactive (non-motile) *E. coli***.

**MAYA:** And how long they survive.

**ALEX:** They're inactivated in **10 minutes at 50–60 °C**. They survive about **10 days in dry dust**, **more than 2 weeks on linen**, **about 9 days in water at 7–10 °C**, **1–2 weeks in food**, and **up to 2 months in ice**.

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:**
- The subgroups are defined by the structure of the **O antigen** (LPS). Some strains also have **K-type surface structures**.
- Within the 4 species/subgroups, types are defined by **numbered antigenic factors**. In ***S. flexneri*** some types are split into subtypes by a "group" antigen shared by several types. Typing uses **polyvalent species sera and type sera**, and for *S. flexneri* **absorbed group sera**.
- ***S. dysenteriae* type 1 also makes a heat-labile protein exotoxin, the Shiga toxin.**

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:**
- Pathogenic through **multiplication and invasiveness**, and in *S. shiga* also through **toxin production**.

**MAYA:** The four stages of invasion.

**ALEX:**
1. **Non-receptor-specific attachment** to the host cell, with changes in the cytoskeleton. An adhesin has been reported only inconsistently, but the ***ipaD* plasmid is always present**.
2. **Plasmid-encoded IpaB and IpaC proteins induce endocytosis** of the bacterium. **Lysis of the phagosome** then allows invasion and **active multiplication in the cytoplasm**.
3. **IcsA and IcsB proteins promote intercellular spread**. "Ics" stands for *intercellular spread*.
4. **Cell death by inhibition of protein synthesis**, by a mechanism not yet understood.

The pathogenesis of the **colonic mucosal ulcerations is the same as for EIEC**.

- **Endotoxin** (O antigen): an **irritant action** in the intestine.
- **Shiga toxin** (produced by *S. dysenteriae* and released by bacteriolysis):
  - it has **no role in invasion**, but it does have a role in **vascular pathology**;
  - it **blocks protein synthesis**;
  - by its cytotoxic effect on **capillary endothelium**, it causes the **vascular complications and hemolytic uremic syndrome** of shigellosis;
  - it also affects the **CNS**: meningism, loss of consciousness, coma;
  - it's lethal in experimental animals.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:**
- **Bacillary dysentery**, a specifically human disease characterized by a **muco-bloody diarrheal syndrome**. It rarely becomes invasive, but in marked biological decline it can cause septicemia.
- **Very low infective dose: only 100 bacteria.** **Incubation 2–5 days.**
- **Sudden onset**: fever, watery diarrhea and intense abdominal pain. Then **very frequent stools (20–40 a day), non-fecal, with mucus, pus and blood**, intestinal colic and **rectal tenesmus**. There are **abscesses** in the wall of the large intestine and terminal ileum, progressing to ulceration, necrosis and hemorrhage.
- **The main problem is fluid and electrolyte loss and acute dehydration.** Without rehydration it can be fatal.

**MAYA:** Severity by species.

**ALEX:**
- ***S. sonnei***: milder, shorter forms;
- ***S. flexneri***: more severe forms;
- ***S. dysenteriae***: **large epidemics**, with disease of particular severity.

**MAYA:** Complications?

**ALEX:** Extraintestinal: **neurological disturbances, hemolytic uremic syndrome and reactive arthritis**. *S. flexneri* has also been reported as a cause of **keratitis**. *Shigella* can also cause infectious-type food poisoning.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

#### 1. Collection and transport

**ALEX:**
- It's a **diagnostic emergency**, because of the implications for the patient and for public health.
- Specimen: **feces** (possibly vomit). Collect **as soon as possible after onset**, when **10³–10⁹ bacteria/g** are shed initially, and before antibiotics.
- Macroscopically: **small amounts of stool with mucus, pus and blood**. Sometimes the blood isn't visible. **Collect and culture especially the fragments containing mucus, pus and blood.**
- Ideally, **inoculate at the bedside**. Otherwise use **Cary-Blair**.

**MAYA:** And the alternative collection methods.

**ALEX:**
- **The Nelaton catheter** is inserted into the sigmoid colon, **10–15 cm in children and 15–20 cm in adults**, and the specimen is aspirated with a syringe. It's useful in atypical cases or for checking carrier status.
- **Rectosigmoidoscopy.**
- Feces are suspended in saline or Cary-Blair.

#### 2. Macro and micro

**ALEX:**
- Macroscopically: **muco-purulent, bloody stool in small amounts**.
- Microscopically: a **fresh wet mount between slide and coverslip** (coprocytology, or the coprocytogram). **NO smears are made.** Examine with the **40× objective**. **Numerous PMNs** support an invasive mechanism. **Over 75% PMNs, together with red cells, suggests bacterial dysentery.** It's a fast, cheap and very useful test.
- Some authors recommend **direct immunofluorescence**, which is expensive because there are so many serotypes.

#### 3. Culture

**ALEX:**
- *Shigella* grows on **weakly and moderately selective media**. It does **NOT multiply on highly selective media**, and it's **partly inhibited in enrichment media**.

**MAYA:** So no enrichment.

**ALEX:** Right. **Isolation is direct**, by plating on weakly and moderately selective media (**MacConkey, ADCL, XLD, SS**) **without prior enrichment**. That's the opposite of *Salmonella*.
- When lactose-negative colonies appear, **pick 3–5 isolated colonies** and subculture them onto multitest media (TSI, MIU, MILF) or API strips.

#### 4. Identification

**4a. Morphology.** Gram-negative rods, 0.5–0.8 by 2–3 µm, **non-motile**, non-capsulated, non-sporulating.

**4b. Culture.** **S-type, lactose-negative, semi-transparent colonies of 1–2 mm** on MacConkey or ADCL. *S. sonnei* can produce colonies that **turn pink** because it ferments lactose late. **Red on XLD.** A typical "lactose-negative" group.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- TSI: glucose + **WITHOUT gas**, lactose/sucrose –, **H₂S –**.
- MIU/MILF: **non-motile**, indole **variable**, **urease –**, **LDC –**, PAD –.
- **Simmons citrate –**.

**MAYA:** Versus *Escherichia* (Table 31-8)?

**ALEX:** *Shigella* produces **no gas from glucose**, **no lysine decarboxylase**, and it's **non-motile**. **Inactive *E. coli* is very hard to separate and needs anti-*Shigella* sera.**

**MAYA:** Separating the subgroups.

**ALEX:** **Mannitol fermentation.** **Subgroup A (*S. dysenteriae*) is mannitol-negative**, and the rest are mannitol-positive.

**MAYA:** And the 6-test table, 31-9.

**ALEX:** Indole, ODC, mannitol, xylose, rhamnose, ONPG:
- ***S. dysenteriae***: **all negative**. Exceptions: indole in serovars 2, 7, 8; xylose in 8, 10; rhamnose in 2, 7; **ONPG + in *S. dysenteriae* 1**.
- ***S. flexneri***: indole d, **mannitol +**, xylose – (except serovars 2, 4, 9, 12, 13, 15), rhamnose –, ODC – (except serovar 6).
- ***S. boydii***: indole d, mannitol +, ODC – (except serovar 13), rhamnose – (except serovars 2, 4, 9, 12, 13, 18).
- ***S. sonnei***: **ODC +, mannitol +, rhamnose +, ONPG +**. Those are the traits that set it clearly apart.

**ALEX:** **The 6 tests can't draw a sharp line between species.** **Serological typing** resolved these borderline cases, and additional biochemical tests may be needed.

**4d. Antigens.**
- **Polyvalent subgroup sera and type-specific sera**, by **slide agglutination**, according to the established schemes (the *Shigella* antigenic scheme, Buiuc 31-13). For *S. flexneri*, **absorbed group sera**.
- If biochemistry suggests *Shigella* but agglutination is negative, **keep a dense suspension 30–60 minutes at 100 °C**, then repeat. Heat removes the K-type envelope that masks O.
- **Latex agglutination** is a rapid screening method with **remarkable results in *Shigella***, which is present in large numbers in stool during the acute phase. It detects **10^4.5 organisms/ml**. The sera can be adsorbed onto ***S. aureus* with protein A** (coagglutination), onto **charcoal particles (India ink)**, or onto latex. It works **directly on stool and on colonies from isolation media**.

**4e. Pathogenicity: the Sereny test.**

**ALEX:** It produces **keratoconjunctivitis in the guinea pig**. Subculture the suspect colony on a slant. **After 8 hours, put a loopful of culture under the guinea pig's eyelid.** After **12–24 hours**, a positive reaction shows **conjunctival congestion, purulent discharge and corneal opacity**, progressing to keratoconjunctivitis.

**MAYA:** And the warning.

**ALEX:** **The test can also be positive with some *E. coli* strains (EIEC).** Invasiveness *in vitro* is shown by **intracellular growth in HeLa cells**.

**4f. Virulence.** *Modern layer*: PCR for ***ipaH***, which is present in **both *Shigella* and EIEC and doesn't separate them**; *ipaB/C*; *icsA*; and PCR for ***stx1*** in *S. dysenteriae* type 1.

**4g. Phage typing.** Used in epidemiological or research studies and reserved for **reference labs**. Schemes were developed especially for the epidemiologically dominant serotypes: ***S. flexneri* 2a and *S. sonnei***. **Phage typing has often been shown superior to biotyping or resistotyping.**

**4h. Other typing.**
- **Biotyping** is little used, given the genus's **modest metabolic activity**. **ONPG and ornithine decarboxylase** work as a **screening system to separate *S. sonnei*** from the other serovars (dysenteriae, flexneri, boydii), which are **consistently inactive on these two tests**. It applies only in outbreaks where *S. sonnei* is already known to be the cause.
- **Bacteriocin typing (colicin typing)** was promoted for *S. sonnei*. The **lability of the preparations and the instability of the bacteriocin types** reduced interest in it.
- **Resistotyping** is one of the most accessible methods, recommended and often used in epidemiological investigations. It's limited by its dependence on many technical variables and by variation in the spectrum even within one outbreak. **Plasmid profiling with identification of resistance plasmids replaces it, with clearly better results.**

**4i. New-generation methods.**
- Molecular biology: **plasmid profiling, ribotyping, DNA probes**.
- *Modern layer*: **MALDI-TOF does not separate *Shigella* from *E. coli***, so identification is **confirmed serologically**. Multiplex PCR (GI panels) detects the shared ***ipaH*** target, reported as "*Shigella*/EIEC." **WGS/cgMLST** is used for outbreaks, including **MDR clones transmitted sexually among men who have sex with men**.

#### 5. Susceptibility testing

**ALEX:**
- **Standardized disk diffusion**, both for **resistance surveillance** and for **choosing treatment**. The antibiogram has become necessary because resistant strains have emerged.
- As with *Salmonella*: **aminoglycosides and first- and second-generation cephalosporins are not reported as susceptible.**
- **Frequent resistance to ampicillin and cotrimoxazol.** For example, the course mentions an ***S. sonnei* strain resistant to ampicillin and cotrimoxazole identified in 2005 in the USA**. Resistance to **ciprofloxacin and azithromycin** is increasingly frequent, and so are **ESBLs (CTX-M-15)**.

### Serological diagnosis

**ALEX:**
- **Serological diagnosis is possible**, by detecting a **rising antibody titer** with tube agglutination, but it has **no practical importance**.
- The infection stimulates a humoral response, but **the antibodies are not protective**. **Local IgA antibodies**, possibly after live attenuated vaccines, might be useful for prevention.

### Treatment and prevention

**ALEX:**
- **Bacterial dysentery is one of the few bacterial acute diarrheal diseases that MUST be treated with antibiotics**, together with fluid and electrolyte rebalancing. Options: **ciprofloxacin, ampicillin/amoxicillin, or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole**.
- **Do NOT give drugs that inhibit intestinal peristalsis.** They **prolong the diarrhea, the fever and the excretion period**.

**MAYA:** Epidemiology.

**ALEX:**
- **Source**: the sick human, symptomatic or not.
- **Fecal-oral transmission**. Remember the **"4 F's"**. In the Romanian course they're the "4 M's": *mâini murdare, muşte, mâncare, materii fecale*, which means **dirty hands, flies, food, feces**. Transmission by **sexual contact** is also possible.
- **The ratio of foodborne to waterborne outbreaks is about 2 to 1.**
- **Children under 10** are mainly affected, **especially ages 0–5**.
- Poor sanitation drives spread.

### Recap

**MAYA:**
- Four subgroups: A *dysenteriae* (mannitol –, type 1 = Shiga toxin), B *flexneri* (2a dominant), C *boydii*, D *sonnei* (ODC +, ONPG +, rhamnose +, late lactose).
- Non-motile, no gas, LDC –, H₂S –, urease –, citrate –. Very close to inactive *E. coli*.
- Dose about 100, incubation 2–5 days, 20–40 stools a day with mucus, pus and blood, tenesmus.
- Invasion via IpaB/C endocytosis, phagosome lysis, IcsA/B spread, cell death. Shiga toxin causes vascular damage, HUS and CNS effects.
- Stool: culture the mucus and blood flecks, bedside inoculation or Cary-Blair, Nelaton catheter. Wet mount with over 75% PMNs. **Direct plating, no enrichment.**
- Slide agglutination; if negative, boil 30–60 minutes. Latex 10^4.5/ml. Sereny test, also positive for EIEC. MALDI-TOF can't tell it from *E. coli*.
- **Treat with antibiotics**, no antiperistaltic drugs.
