### Annex 3 — Orientation key: fermenters vs non-fermenters

**MAYA:** This is the big summary grid. It covers Enterobacterales, *V. cholerae*, *Aeromonas*, *Plesiomonas*, *P. aeruginosa*, *A. baumannii*, *S. maltophilia* and *C. jejuni*.

**ALEX:** Row by row:

- **Glucose metabolism**: fermentative for Enterobacterales, *Vibrio*, *Aeromonas* and *Plesiomonas*. **Oxidative** for *P. aeruginosa*, *A. baumannii* (asaccharolytic in other species) and *S. maltophilia*. ***Campylobacter* doesn't attack carbohydrates at all.**
- **Oxidase**: **negative** for Enterobacterales, ***A. baumannii*** and ***S. maltophilia***. **Positive** for *Vibrio, Aeromonas, Plesiomonas, P. aeruginosa, C. jejuni*.
- **Catalase**: positive for all.
- **Motility and flagella**: Enterobacterales are peritrichous or non-motile. *Vibrio, Aeromonas, Plesiomonas* are polar monotrichous. *P. aeruginosa* has 1–3 polar flagella. ***A. baumannii* is non-motile and non-flagellated.** ***S. maltophilia* is polar lophotrichous.** *C. jejuni* has a single polar flagellum, with darting motility.
- **Growth on MacConkey**: yes for Enterobacterales, *Aeromonas*, *Plesiomonas*, *P. aeruginosa* (lactose –), *A. baumannii* (lactose –) and *S. maltophilia*. Only some *Vibrio* species. ***C. jejuni* doesn't.**
- **Atmosphere**: Enterobacterales, *Vibrio*, *Aeromonas* and *Plesiomonas* are aerobic and facultatively anaerobic. *P. aeruginosa* is strictly aerobic (except nitrate respiration). *A. baumannii* and *S. maltophilia* are strictly aerobic. ***C. jejuni* is microaerophilic, 5% O₂.**
- **Diagnostically useful temperature**: 35–37 °C for Enterobacterales. 35–37 °C and pH 8.6–9.5 for *Vibrio*. 35–37 °C for *Aeromonas*. 8–45 °C for *Plesiomonas*. ***P. aeruginosa* grows at 42 °C but not 4 °C.** ***A. baumannii* grows at 44 °C.** 35 °C for *S. maltophilia*. ***C. jejuni* grows at 42 °C but not at 25 °C.**
- **Selective medium of choice**: MacConkey, ADCL, XLD, SS for Enterobacterales. TCBS, BSA and APW for *Vibrio*. CIN blood agar or ampicillin for *Aeromonas*. MacConkey for *Plesiomonas*. *Pseudomonas* base agar + cetrimide for *P. aeruginosa*. LAM or CHROMagar *Acinetobacter* for *A. baumannii*. **VIA or imipenem agar for *S. maltophilia*.** Skirrow, CCDA or Preston under microaerophilia for *C. jejuni*.
- **Pigment**: rare in Enterobacterales (*Serratia* red, *Enterobacter* yellow). None for *Vibrio*. Brown for *A. salmonicida*. None for *Plesiomonas*. Pyocyanin plus pyoverdin for *P. aeruginosa*. None for *A. baumannii*. **Yellow-greenish for *S. maltophilia*.** None for *C. jejuni*.

### Annex 5 — The diarrheagenic *E. coli* pathotypes

**ALEX:** Covered in episode 2. One extra from the annex: **identifying pathotypes (Buiuc 31-12)** combines four approaches:
- ***in vivo*** methods: ligated ileal loop or newborn mouse for ETEC, piglet for EHEC, Sereny guinea pig test for EIEC;
- **serogroup assignment**;
- **cytopathic effect on cell cultures**: Vero for EHEC, HeLa for EIEC, EAggEC and DAEC, Hep-2 for EPEC;
- **genetic (PCR)** for toxin and adhesin genes, plus **immunological** methods: latex, ELISA, the Biken immunodiffusion test, and Vero seroneutralization.

### Annex 6 — Natural resistances (EUCAST expected phenotypes)

**MAYA:** The whole table is an addition beyond the course materials, right?

**ALEX:** Yes, the whole table. **Its purpose is quality control: a strain reported "susceptible" to an antibiotic its species is naturally resistant to signals an identification or testing error.**

- ***E. coli***: practically none. **Any resistance is acquired**: ESBL CTX-M-15 (ST131), plasmid AmpC, carbapenemases, *mcr-1*.
- ***Klebsiella pneumoniae/oxytoca***: ampicillin, amoxicillin, ticarcillin (SHV-1/OXY). **Never report "ampicillin susceptible."**
- ***Enterobacter, C. freundii, Serratia, Morganella, Providencia, Hafnia***: aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, first-generation cephalosporins, cefoxitin (**inducible AmpC**). There's a **derepression risk with third-generation cephalosporins, so prefer cefepime or carbapenems** in severe infections.
- ***C. koseri***: aminopenicillins, ticarcillin (penicillinase). It **stays susceptible to amoxicillin-clavulanate and first-generation cephalosporins**.
- ***Proteus* (all species)**: tetracyclines, tigecycline, colistin, nitrofurantoin. **No nitrofurantoin for *Proteus* UTIs.**
- ***P. vulgaris, P. penneri***: also aminopenicillins and cefuroxime (class A "K1"). ***P. mirabilis* is ampicillin-susceptible.**
- ***Morganella, Providencia***: colistin, tetracyclines, tigecycline, nitrofurantoin. ***P. stuartii/P. rettgeri* are also resistant to gentamicin and tobramycin** (AAC(2′)-Ia). **Amikacin usually stays active.**
- ***Serratia marcescens***: also colistin. **Don't use colistin.**
- ***Salmonella, Shigella***: aminoglycosides, first- and second-generation cephalosporins and cephamycins look active *in vitro* but are **clinically ineffective**, because of intracellular survival. **Don't report them as susceptible.** Test ciprofloxacin (pefloxacin screen), ceftriaxone, azithromycin, cotrimoxazole.
- ***Y. enterocolitica***: aminopenicillins, ticarcillin, first-generation cephalosporins (β-lactamases A and B). Active: cotrimoxazole, FQs, third-generation cephalosporins, aminoglycosides.
- ***Campylobacter jejuni/coli***: trimethoprim, sulfonamides, vancomycin, rifampicin, aztreonam. **Drug of choice: azithromycin.** FQ resistance from *gyrA* is over 50% in some countries.
- ***Aeromonas***: ampicillin (except *A. trota*), amoxicillin-clavulanate. Multiple inducible β-lactamases including **CphA carbapenemase**. **Avoid β-lactam monotherapy.**
- ***P. aeruginosa***: aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, first- and second-generation cephalosporins, **cefotaxime/ceftriaxone, ertapenem**, tetracyclines, tigecycline, trimethoprim, cotrimoxazole, chloramphenicol. **Active**: ceftazidime, cefepime, piperacillin-tazobactam, imipenem/meropenem, aztreonam, aminoglycosides, ciprofloxacin, colistin.
- ***A. baumannii***: aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, first- and second-generation cephalosporins, **aztreonam, ertapenem**, trimethoprim, chloramphenicol, fosfomycin (ADC + OXA-51-like + impermeability). Options: **sulbactam (± durlobactam), colistin, cefiderocol, minocycline**.
- ***S. maltophilia***: **all carbapenems**, most β-lactams, aminoglycosides, aztreonam (**L1 MBL + L2 cephalosporinase**). **Drug of choice: cotrimoxazole**, with minocycline and levofloxacin as alternatives.
- ***B. cepacia* complex**: aminoglycosides, colistin, aminopenicillins, first- and second-generation cephalosporins. Active: cotrimoxazole, meropenem, ceftazidime, minocycline.

### Annex 7 — Rapid review sheet

**ALEX:** For each genus, five things: specimen, isolation medium, key identification trait, typing, serology. Let me go fast.

- ***E. coli***: urine (quantitative), stool, blood, CSF · MacConkey, EMB; SMAC for O157 · lactose +, indole +, citrate –, LDC +, sorbitol – in O157 · phage typing O157, O26, O55, O111; RFLP, MLST, ribotyping · **no serology**.
- ***Klebsiella***: redcurrant-jelly sputum, urine, blood · MacConkey · non-motile + capsulated + urease + + VP + + LDC +; *K. oxytoca* indole + · Slopek-Milch + Popovici phage typing (Cantacuzino); plasmid spectrum · **no serology**.
- ***Enterobacter***: blood, CSF, tracheal aspirate · MacConkey · motile, lactose +, VP +, ODC +, LDC – (except *E. aerogenes*) · phage typing abandoned; plasmid spectrum · **no**.
- ***Serratia***: urine, blood, bronchial aspirate · MacConkey (lactose –) · red prodigiosin at 48–72 h, DNase +, gelatinase +, VP + · serotyping (over 70 serovars, H antigen by 6‰ phenol); phage typing not widespread · **no**.
- ***Citrobacter***: stool, urine, blood, neonatal CSF · MacConkey, XLD (yellow) · gas +, H₂S or indole +, LDC –, citrate +, KCN +; agglutinates anti-*Salmonella* O and Vi · phage and bacteriocin typing for *C. diversus* · **no**.
- ***Proteus***: urine, pus, wounds · MacConkey, ADCL (inhibit swarming) · swarming + urease + + PAD + + H₂S +; Dienes; climbing · weak phage typing; Dienes line · **no (Weil-Felix is for rickettsiae)**.
- ***Morganella***: post-operative urine, wounds · MacConkey · PAD +, urease +, indole +, ODC +, citrate –, no swarming · resistotyping · **no**.
- ***Providencia***: urine (chronic catheters), wounds · MacConkey · PAD +, indole +, citrate +, malonate +, ODC –, no swarming · *P. rettgeri* biotyping (rhamnose, salicin); resistotyping · **no**.
- ***Salmonella***: stool; blood in week 1 of typhoid, then urine, bile, marrow · selenite or Müller-Kauffmann + MacConkey + ADCL/XLD; Wilson-Blair for carriers · lactose –, H₂S +, citrate +, LDC +, indole –, urease –; Typhi has no gas, citrate –, ODC – · Typhi phage typing (over 100 lysovars, Vi receptor), Paratyphi B, Typhimurium, Enteritidis; biotyping; IS200; PFGE; ribotyping · **YES: TO, AO, BO; d, a, b; 1/250 (O), 1/2,500 (H); anti-Vi over 1/40 in carriers**.
- ***Shigella***: stool (mucus, pus and blood flecks) · MacConkey, ADCL, XLD, SS; no enrichment; Cary-Blair · non-motile + no gas + LDC – + lactose –; mannitol – in subgroup A · phage typing *S. flexneri* 2a and *S. sonnei*; biotyping (ONPG, ODC); colicin typing · **possible, but of no practical importance**.
- ***Yersinia***: stool, lymph nodes, blood; bubo pus, sputum (*Y. pestis*, BSL-3/4) · CIN, MacConkey at 48 h; cold enrichment at 4 °C for 1–3 weeks · motile at 22–30 °C, non-motile at 37 °C (*pestis* always non-motile), urease +, citrate –, no gas; bull's-eye on CIN · phage typing replaced by genetics; pesticins I and II; REAP/REAC, PFGE, ribotyping + PCR · **YES: tube agglutination ≥1/160, paired sera (reactive arthritis, erythema nodosum); plague over 1/26, microagglutination/CFT/ELISA**.
- ***V. cholerae***: rice-water stool, vomit; Cary-Blair or APW · APW (6–8 h) → TCBS (yellow) + BSA · oxidase + + string test + + O/129 sensitive + grows at pH 8.6 · 146 lysovars (extended Basu-Mukerjee set); biovar + serovar · **no (antitoxin antibodies don't mean protection)**.
- ***Aeromonas / Plesiomonas***: stool, wounds, blood (cirrhotics) · CIN blood agar, ampicillin; MacConkey for *Plesiomonas* · oxidase + but O/129- and ampicillin-resistant (*Aeromonas*), string test –, no growth at 6% NaCl · O serogroups, ribotyping · **no**.
- ***Campylobacter***: stool (Cary-Blair), blood cultures for *C. fetus* · Skirrow, CCDA, Preston, microaerophilic, 42 °C, 48 h; 0.45–0.65 µm filtration · gull wings, darting motility, oxidase +, catalase +, hippurate + in *C. jejuni* · Penner/Lior serotyping; *flaA*-RFLP, PFGE, MLST · **not routine; ELISA only retrospectively (Guillain-Barré, reactive arthritis)**.
- ***H. pylori***: gastric biopsy (antrum + body); breath, stool, serum · chocolate or Brucella agar with 5–7% horse blood, microaerophilic, 2–7 days · very strong urease, catalase +, oxidase +, nitrates –, hippurate –; Giemsa imprints · *cagA/vacA* genotyping; MLST, PFGE · **YES, as a guide: ELISA IgG, IgA, IgM; can't separate active from cured infection, so not used to confirm eradication**.
- ***P. aeruginosa***: blue-green fluorescent pus, sputum, urine, blood, ear or eye discharge · *Pseudomonas* base agar + cetrimide, Irgasan, C-N, C-F-C; blood agar · oxidase + + pigment (pyocyanin, pyoverdin) + linden or jasmine smell + 42 °C + oxidizes glucose · sero-, phage and pyocin typing; restriction endonucleases, DNA probes, PCR · **no (insignificant titers, no protection)**.
- ***A. baumannii***: tracheal aspirate or BAL, blood culture, urine, wound, catheter tip · blood agar, MacConkey; LAM or CHROMagar *Acinetobacter*; 44 °C · coccobacilli in pairs + non-motile + OXIDASE – + non-fermenter + grows on MacConkey · MLST (Oxford/Pasteur), IC1–IC3; PFGE; PCR *bla*OXA-51-like = species marker · **no**.

## THE RAPID-FIRE QUIZ

**ALEX:** Okay, Maya. Thirty questions, one-word or one-line answers. Go.

**ALEX:** 1. Four defining biochemical traits of Enterobacterales?
**MAYA:** Ferment glucose, oxidase-negative, catalase-positive, reduce nitrates to nitrites.

**ALEX:** 2. More than what percentage of normal gut flora is anaerobic?
**MAYA:** Over 90%.

**ALEX:** 3. Which antigen is heat-stable and agglutinates slowly and granularly?
**MAYA:** O. H is heat-labile and agglutinates fast and fluffy.

**ALEX:** 4. How are H suspensions preserved?
**MAYA:** 5‰ formalin. Except *Serratia*, where H antigens are prepared with 6‰ phenol.

**ALEX:** 5. Name the six diarrheagenic *E. coli* pathotypes.
**MAYA:** ETEC, EHEC/STEC, EIEC, EAggEC, EPEC, DAEC.

**ALEX:** 6. Which medium for O157, and what do its colonies look like?
**MAYA:** Sorbitol MacConkey. Colorless, sorbitol-negative colonies.

**ALEX:** 7. Why no antibiotics in EHEC?
**MAYA:** They induce the lambdoid phage, cause massive toxin release and raise the risk of HUS.

**ALEX:** 8. Urine transport rule?
**MAYA:** 30 minutes, otherwise +4 °C.

**ALEX:** 9. The UTI threshold?
**MAYA:** ≥10⁵ CFU/ml. Or 10⁴–10⁵ with leukocyturia and symptoms. Or any count from a suprapubic puncture.

**ALEX:** 10. The only *Klebsiella* that's indole-positive?
**MAYA:** *K. oxytoca*.

**ALEX:** 11. The only malonate-negative *Klebsiella*?
**MAYA:** *K. ozaenae*.

**ALEX:** 12. What does a string test over 5 mm mean?
**MAYA:** Hypermucoviscous, hypervirulent *K. pneumoniae*.

**ALEX:** 13. *Cronobacter sakazakii*: source and mortality?
**MAYA:** Powdered infant formula. 40–80% mortality from neonatal meningitis or necrotizing enterocolitis.

**ALEX:** 14. Which *Serratia* trait trap mimics *S.* Typhi?
**MAYA:** On TSI/MILF: no or little gas, no H₂S, lactose –, motile, indole –, LDC +, PAD –. You separate them with ODC, DNase, gelatinase, VP, adonitol, sorbitol and arabinose.

**ALEX:** 15. Three *Proteus* culture phenomena?
**MAYA:** Swarming in concentric waves, the Dienes demarcation line, and the climbing phenomenon in condensation water.

**ALEX:** 16. *P. mirabilis* versus *P. vulgaris* on indole, ODC and maltose?
**MAYA:** *Mirabilis*: indole –, ODC +, maltose –. *Vulgaris*: the opposite. And the scanned table in the book has those rows swapped.

**ALEX:** 17. *Morganella* versus *Providencia*?
**MAYA:** *Morganella* is ODC + and citrate –. *Providencia* is ODC –, citrate + and malonate +. Neither swarms.

**ALEX:** 18. Specimen chronology in typhoid?
**MAYA:** Blood in week one. Then stool, urine, bile and bone marrow.

**ALEX:** 19. The *S.* Typhi antigenic formula?
**MAYA:** O 9,12,Vi; H d; no phase 2. Group D1.

**ALEX:** 20. Widal-type tube series and suggestive titers?
**MAYA:** TO, AO, BO and d, a, b. 52 °C, 18–20 h for O and 2 h for H. 1/250 for O, 1/2,500 for H, a fourfold rise 7–10 days apart. Anti-Vi over 1/40 in carriers. Useless in vaccinated people.

**ALEX:** 21. Three typhoid vaccines?
**MAYA:** Vi polysaccharide i.m., oral live Ty21a, and the inactivated whole-cell vaccine, which is the most reactogenic. 50–80% protection.

**ALEX:** 22. *Shigella* infective dose, and do we enrich?
**MAYA:** About 100 bacteria. No enrichment, we plate directly.

**ALEX:** 23. Which *Shigella* subgroup is mannitol-negative?
**MAYA:** Subgroup A, *S. dysenteriae*.

**ALEX:** 24. *Yersinia* motility rule?
**MAYA:** Motile at 22–30 °C, non-motile at 37 °C. *Y. pestis* is always non-motile.

**ALEX:** 25. The *Y. pestis* colony, and the *Y. enterocolitica* colony on CIN?
**MAYA:** *Y. pestis* makes "Chinese hat" colonies. On CIN you get a bull's eye with a red center.

**ALEX:** 26. Cholera stool microscopy: what do you NOT see?
**MAYA:** Leukocytes. Cholera isn't invasive.

**ALEX:** 27. *V. cholerae* on TCBS, and why you don't do oxidase straight from TCBS?
**MAYA:** Yellow colonies. Sucrose on TCBS can give a false-positive oxidase.

**ALEX:** 28. *Aeromonas* versus *Vibrio*, in three tests?
**MAYA:** *Aeromonas* is O/129-resistant, doesn't grow at 6% NaCl, and is string-test negative.

**ALEX:** 29. *C. jejuni* versus *C. coli*?
**MAYA:** Hippurate hydrolysis is positive in *jejuni*.

**ALEX:** 30. Why can't serology confirm *H. pylori* eradication, and what do you use instead?
**MAYA:** Antibodies persist for months or years. Use the urea breath test or stool antigen at least 4 weeks after treatment, and 2 weeks off PPIs.

**ALEX:** Bonus round. What's the single test that separates *Acinetobacter* from *Pseudomonas*?
**MAYA:** Oxidase. *Acinetobacter* is negative, *Pseudomonas* is positive. And *Acinetobacter* is non-motile.

**ALEX:** And the drug of choice for *Stenotrophomonas*?
**MAYA:** Cotrimoxazole, because the L1 metallo-β-lactamase kills every carbapenem.

**ALEX:** And what does Weil-Felix diagnose?
**MAYA:** Rickettsial diseases, not *Proteus*.

**ALEX:** Thirty out of thirty, plus the bonus. You're ready.

**MAYA:** Thank you, Alex. And thanks to everyone listening to *The Gram-Negative Rounds*. Go back to the episode for any genus you stumbled on, and good luck on the exam.

**ALEX:** Don't plate *Shigella* on Wilson-Blair, and don't give nitrofurantoin for *Proteus*. Goodbye!
