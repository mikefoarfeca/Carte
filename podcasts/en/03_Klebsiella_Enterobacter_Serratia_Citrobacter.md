## PART A — *KLEBSIELLA*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** The type species was described in 1885 as *Bakterium lactis aerogenes*, and the current name dates from 1954. The genus has 10 species. The medically important ones are ***K. pneumoniae*, *K. oxytoca*, *K. ozaenae* and *K. rhinoscleromatis***.

**MAYA:** And the modern changes?

**ALEX:** *K. ozaenae* and *K. rhinoscleromatis* are now **subspecies of *K. pneumoniae***: subsp. *ozaenae* and subsp. *rhinoscleromatis*. ***Enterobacter aerogenes* has been reclassified as *Klebsiella aerogenes***. The *K. pneumoniae* complex also includes *K. variicola* and *K. quasipneumoniae*. And some strains have moved to the genus ***Raoultella***.

### Habitat

**ALEX:** It's widespread in nature, in soil and water, and also in human and animal intestines and the upper respiratory flora. In hospitals, **multiresistant strains are selected** that colonize the urinary or respiratory tract in adults and the intestinal tract in children, especially after prolonged broad-spectrum antibiotics.

### General characters

**ALEX:**
- **Capsulated** Gram-negative rods. The capsule is **2–3 times wider than the bacterium itself**.
- **Non-motile**, non-sporulating, sometimes arranged in pairs.
- Intense carbohydrate metabolism, producing acid and **large amounts of gas**, except *K. rhinoscleromatis*. They ferment most sugars, **except dulcitol**.
- They produce acetoin (**VP +**), **lysine decarboxylase** and **urease**. They grow on **Simmons citrate**. **No H₂S, no phenylalanine deaminase.**

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:**
- **O somatic antigens** (LPS), group-specific.
- **K capsular antigens**, polysaccharide and type-specific. There are **over 80 types**, identified by the **capsular swelling reaction**, agglutination, coagglutination or latex agglutination. The Kauffmann and Ørskov scheme has 77 serovars. Because there are so many serovars and they're antigenically related, typing is laborious.

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:**
- **The capsule (K antigen) is the main pathogenicity factor**. It gives resistance to phagocytosis.
- **Endotoxin** (the O antigen), released when the bacterium is destroyed.
- It's conditionally pathogenic and acts through **multiplication and invasiveness**.
- **Type 1 and type 3 fimbriae**, for adherence to uroepithelium and catheters and for biofilm formation. Siderophores: enterobactin, yersiniabactin, aerobactin, salmochelin.

**MAYA:** And the hypervirulent strains.

**ALEX:** That's the *modern layer*. **Hypervirulent *K. pneumoniae* (hvKp)** is often K1 or K2, with genes *rmpA/rmpA2* and *magA*, and a **hypermucoviscous** phenotype. The **"string test"** is positive when touching a colony with a loop pulls a filament longer than **5 mm**. These strains cause cryptogenic pyogenic **liver abscesses**, **endophthalmitis** and **meningitis**, in people **without immunosuppression**, especially in Southeast Asia.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:**
- **Pneumonia.** Only **1–3% of pneumonias**, but **extremely severe**, necrotizing and hemorrhagic, especially in people with poor defenses: the elderly, chronic lung disease, diabetics and alcoholics. The sputum is mucoid, dark red, **"redcurrant jelly."**
- **Other infections**: urinary infections, enteritis (especially in children), food poisoning, wound and postoperative infections, and localized or generalized septic complications. ***Klebsiella* septicemia has a severe prognosis.**
- *K. pneumoniae* and *K. oxytoca* are among the most frequent enterobacteria in **nosocomial infections**.
- ***K. ozaenae*** is isolated from the nasal mucosa of patients with **ozena**, a progressive atrophy of the mucosa with loss of smell. ***K. rhinoscleromatis*** causes **rhinoscleroma**, a destructive granuloma of the nose and pharynx. Both are **exceptionally rare** in ENT practice in Romania.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1. Collection.** Depends on site: sputum for pneumonia, urine, feces, blood, pus, bronchial secretions, tracheal aspirate. General rules apply.

**2. Macro and micro.**
- Macroscopically: mucoid, dark red **"redcurrant jelly" sputum**.
- Microscopically: **at least two sputum smears**, one stained with **methylene blue** and one with **Gram**, examined under oil immersion. Note the alveolar cells (macrophages), inflammatory cells (leukocytes), and relatively large Gram-negative rods with a big capsule. **With methylene blue, the capsule shows up as a halo** around the bacteria.
- The microscopy has to show that the specimen is acceptable (sputum validation criteria) and give a presumptive identification.
- Rapid direct identification on the specimen is possible with the **capsular swelling reaction** using anti-capsular antibodies.

**3. Culture.**
- It grows easily on routine media in 18–24 h at 35–37 °C. **Weakly selective** media like MacConkey are preferred. It **does not grow on highly selective media** and is partly inhibited on moderately selective ones (ADCL, SS, XLD), where colonies are lactose/xylose-positive at 48 h and less mucoid.
- On lactose media: **lactose-positive, large** colonies (2–3 mm at 24 h, over 4 mm at 48 h), domed, viscous and creamy. They look **"like a drop of honey"** and **"flow"** across the surface of the medium, tending to merge.
- Inoculation into susceptible lab animals, the white mouse, is also possible.

**4a. Morphology.** Large, capsulated Gram-negative rods, non-motile, single, in pairs or short chains. The capsule can be lost in culture.

**4b. Culture.** Lactose-positive, M-type colonies, large, domed, viscous, "drop of honey," confluent.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- TSI: glucose + with **abundant gas**, lactose/sucrose + (fermented quickly), H₂S –.
- MIU: **non-motile**, **indole –**, urease +. The exception is ***K. oxytoca*, which is indole-positive**. That's the unique trait that separates it from *K. pneumoniae*.
- Citrate +, VP +, lysine decarboxylase +, PAD –.

**MAYA:** There's a three-trait combination that separates *Klebsiella* from its neighbors.

**ALEX:** **Non-motile + lysine decarboxylase-positive + no phenylalanine deaminase** separates *Klebsiella* from *Enterobacter*, *Hafnia* and *Serratia*.

**MAYA:** The species table, Buiuc 31-21.

**ALEX:** Percent positive:
- ***K. pneumoniae***: indole 0, VP 98, urease 95, ODC 0, LDC 98, malonate 93, ONPG 99.
- ***K. oxytoca***: **indole 99**, VP 95, urease 90, ODC 0, LDC 99, malonate 98, ONPG 100.
- ***K. ozaenae***: indole 0, VP 0, urease 10, ODC 3, LDC 40, **malonate 3**. It's the **only malonate-negative species**. ONPG 80.
- ***K. rhinoscleromatis***: all negative except **malonate 95**, ONPG 0. It can't produce indole, acetoin, urease, ornithine decarboxylase, lysine decarboxylase or β-galactosidase.

Definitive identification uses an extended set of at least 20 characters, conventional or microtest.

**4d. Antigens.** Sera for typing the K antigens (over 80 types) by agglutination, coagglutination or latex, done in **reference labs**.

**4e. Pathogenicity.** Inoculation of the **white mouse**.

**4f. Virulence.** Identifying and studying **virulence plasmids** helps estimate pathogenic activity. *Modern layer*: the string test, and PCR for *rmpA, magA, iuc, iro*.

**4g. Phage typing.** The **Cantacuzino Institute** uses a combined **Slopek-Milch** scheme with the domestic scheme from **Popovici** et al. added. It's **more accessible, simpler and cheaper than serotyping**.

**4h. Other typing.**
- **Resistotyping** has to account for the genus's high and multiple resistance. The source says third-generation cephalosporins, floxacins, colimycin and gentamicin are active on *Klebsiella*. The document notes this should be **re-evaluated against current epidemiology**.
- **Bacteriocin typing.** Genetic typing by **plasmid spectrum**, especially in hospital strains, is one of the routine typing methods for *Klebsiella*.

**4i. New-generation methods.** MALDI-TOF, which doesn't reliably separate *K. pneumoniae* from *K. variicola*. WGS/cgMLST for high-risk clones in carbapenemase outbreaks: **ST258, ST307, ST147, ST11**. Rapid molecular carbapenemase tests directly from positive blood cultures.

**5. Susceptibility.**
- **Mandatory**, usually disk diffusion. MIC and MBC may be needed. *K. pneumoniae* is one of the organisms with **remarkable antibiotic resistance**.
- **Natural resistance to aminopenicillins** (ampicillin, amoxicillin) **and ticarcillin**, through the chromosomal penicillinase **SHV-1** (*K. pneumoniae*) or **OXY** (*K. oxytoca*). So **never report *Klebsiella* as "ampicillin-susceptible."**
- Key acquired phenotypes: **ESBLs** (CTX-M, SHV, TEM), screened with cefpodoxime, cefotaxime or ceftazidime and confirmed by clavulanate synergy; **carbapenemases** KPC, OXA-48, NDM, VIM (KPC-producing *K. pneumoniae* is "KPC-Kp"); AmpC hyperproduction; and colistin resistance (*mgrB* mutations, *mcr*).
- Options for CRE: ceftazidime-avibactam (KPC, OXA-48), meropenem-vaborbactam, imipenem-relebactam, cefiderocol, and aztreonam-avibactam (for MBLs).

### Serology
**ALEX:** **Not done**. Diagnosis is bacteriological.

### Treatment and prevention
**ALEX:** Treatment can start with **gentamicin + cefotaxime**, then continue based on the antibiogram. Prevention: surveillance of hospitalized patients, contact precautions, hand hygiene, limiting broad-spectrum antibiotics, and **rectal screening** for carriage of carbapenemase-producing enterobacteria.

## PART B — *ENTEROBACTER*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** The genus has 16 species. Only two are frequent in hospital infections, ***E. cloacae* and *E. aerogenes***, and 7 others have been isolated occasionally from human disease. The species: *E. cloacae, E. aerogenes, E. agglomerans, E. gergoviae, E. sakazakii, E. taylorae, E. amnigenus, E. asburiae, E. hormaechei*.

**MAYA:** And there's a lot of renaming.

**ALEX:** A lot. *E. aerogenes* → ***Klebsiella aerogenes***. *E. sakazakii* → ***Cronobacter sakazakii***. *E. agglomerans* → ***Pantoea agglomerans***. And *E. cloacae* is really a **complex of species**: *E. cloacae, E. hormaechei, E. asburiae, E. kobei, E. ludwigii, E. roggenkampii*. These can only be separated reliably by molecular methods (*hsp60*) or WGS.

### Habitat

**ALEX:** Environment (soil, water, plants), and the intestinal microbiota of humans and animals. They're part of the biological recycling ecosystems. They're also an important **hospital reservoir**: infusion fluids, parenteral nutrition, equipment.

### General characters

**ALEX:**
- **Motile** (peritrichous) enterobacteria.
- They ferment glucose with **large amounts of gas**, and ferment lactose (β-galactosidase +), rhamnose and sorbitol.
- They produce acetylmethylcarbinol (**VP +**) and **ornithine decarboxylase**.
- **No indole, no H₂S, no PAD.**
- Exception: some biovars of *E. agglomerans* produce no gas.

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:** A serotyping scheme with **60 O factors and 34 H factors** was developed, but it's used only on a limited basis. A phage typing scheme was **abandoned for lack of sensitivity**, because over 20% of strains were untypeable.

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:**
- Conditionally pathogenic; endotoxin; can colonize medical materials.
- **The clinically essential factor is inducible chromosomal AmpC β-lactamase.** Treatment with third-generation cephalosporins selects **derepressed mutants**, so you get treatment failure **during the course of therapy**.
- ***Cronobacter sakazakii*** survives in **dehydrated powdered infant formula** and resists osmotic stress and drying.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:**
- Hospital infections: neonatal sepsis, meningitis, septicemia, UTIs, ventilator-associated pneumonia, catheter infections and wound infections. In nosocomial musculoskeletal and skin infections, *Enterobacter* ranks just after staphylococci and pseudomonads.
- ***Cronobacter sakazakii***: **neonatal meningitis and necrotizing enterocolitis**, linked to **powdered milk formula**, with **40–80% mortality**.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1. Collection.** Blood, CSF, urine, tracheal aspirate, pus, catheters. General rules apply.

**2. Microscopy.** Gram-negative rods with no distinctive features.

**3. Culture.**
- On simple media all species grow quickly, forming **large 2–3 mm round, typical S colonies** in 24 h at 37 °C. Some *E. agglomerans* strains form rough, **"cauliflower-like"** colonies.
- ***E. sakazakii* and *E. agglomerans* consistently produce a characteristic yellow pigment in 2–4 days.**
- On weakly selective media: large lactose-positive colonies. *E. aerogenes* can form large lactose-positive colonies that **look like *Klebsiella***. In principle the genus doesn't form colonies on moderately selective media.

**4a.** Motile, non-sporulating, **non-capsulated** Gram-negative rods.

**4b.** Large S colonies; yellow pigment (*E. sakazakii, E. agglomerans*); lactose-positive.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- TSI: glucose + with gas, lactose/sucrose +, H₂S –.
- MILF: motile, indole –, **lysine decarboxylase + only in *E. aerogenes* and *E. gergoviae***, PAD –.
- VP +, ODC +, citrate +.

**MAYA:** Species differentiation, Buiuc 31-22.

**ALEX:** Percent positive:
- ***E. aerogenes***: LDC 98, ADH 0, ODC 98, sucrose 100, sorbitol 100, adonitol 98, raffinose 96, malonate 95, urease 2.
- ***E. cloacae***: LDC 0, **ADH 97**, ODC 96, sorbitol 95, adonitol 25, urease 65.
- ***E. agglomerans***: LDC 0, ADH 0, ODC 0, **yellow pigment**.
- ***E. gergoviae***: LDC 90, ODC 100, **urease 93**.
- ***E. sakazakii***: ADH 99, ODC 91, **sorbitol 0**, raffinose 99, **yellow pigment**.

Definitive identification is **primarily exoenzymatic**, on extended test sets (Table 31-5). Preliminary assignment is done on TSI-MILF.

**4d.** Serotyping (60 O, 34 H), limited use, specialized labs only.
**4e.** Pathogenicity tests not done routinely.
**4f.** *Modern*: PCR for ***hsp60*** to assign strains to clusters of the *E. cloacae* complex; detection of AmpC genes.
**4g.** Phage typing **abandoned** (over 20% untypeable).
**4h.** **Plasmid spectrum** is one of the typing methods most used to investigate hospital infections caused by *E. aerogenes* and *E. cloacae*. Other genetic methods are used sporadically. **Resistotyping** can be useful, since species vary in susceptibility to floxacins, third-generation cephalosporins and aminoglycosides.
**4i.** MALDI-TOF (with limits inside the *E. cloacae* complex); WGS/cgMLST for outbreaks; *hsp60/rpoB* sequencing.

**5. Susceptibility.**
- Standardized disk diffusion; MIC when needed.
- **Natural resistance**: aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, first-generation cephalosporins, cefoxitin (inducible AmpC). **Cefuroxime is also inactive.**
- **Don't report "susceptible" to third-generation cephalosporins without a warning**, because of the risk of AmpC derepression during treatment. **Cefepime or carbapenems are preferred in severe infections.**
- ESBLs and carbapenemases are increasingly frequent: VIM, NDM, OXA-48, KPC.

### Serology
**ALEX:** Not done.

### Treatment
**ALEX:** According to the antibiogram. In severe infections, carbapenems or cefepime. **Avoid third-generation cephalosporins as monotherapy.**

## PART C — *SERRATIA*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** The genus includes 12 species, 10 of which have been isolated from human infections. The main species is ***S. marcescens***. Others: *S. liquefaciens, S. rubidaea, S. odorifera, S. plymuthica, S. ficaria, S. fonticola*.

**MAYA:** And it's ancient.

**ALEX:** *S. marcescens*, with its characteristic red pigment, was **described in 1823**. Its presence was being discussed about **three centuries BC**. Today the genus belongs to the family ***Yersiniaceae***.

### Habitat

**ALEX:** Environment (water, soil, plants) and biological recycling ecosystems. In hospitals it turns up in solutions, equipment and **diluted disinfectants**.

### General characters

**ALEX:**
- **Motile** enterobacteria that grow luxuriantly on simple and weakly or moderately selective media.
- Metabolically:
  - **small amounts of gas** from glucose; *S. marcescens* is often **anaerogenic**;
  - produce **lysine and ornithine decarboxylases**, **DNase**, **proteases** and **lipases**;
  - **ferment lactose late**, but are **promptly β-galactosidase-positive**;
  - **no indole, no H₂S, no PAD**;
  - **VP +**.

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:** The scheme proposed by **Ewing and Davis** and extended by **Grimont** identifies **over 70 serovars** by O and H agglutination. One unique detail: unlike other enterobacteria, *Serratia* **H antigens are prepared with 6‰ phenol**, not with formalin.

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:** Conditionally pathogenic, with a rich **lipolytic and proteolytic equipment**, **high antibiotic resistance** (especially to cephalosporins), and endotoxin. *S. marcescens* is a recognized urinary and respiratory pathogen and causes serious primary or secondary bacteremia and septicemia.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:** A relatively frequent conditional pathogen in hospital infections: urinary, respiratory, bacteremia, septicemia, catheter infections, **endophthalmitis** and wound infections. Together with *E. coli*, it ranks just after staphylococci and pseudomonads in nosocomial musculoskeletal and skin infections. It's **almost exclusively extraintestinal**.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1. Collection.** Urine, blood, bronchial aspirate, pus, catheters, and **suspect infusion fluids** (outbreak investigation).

**2.** Gram-negative rods on the smear. Macroscopically, rarely, red pigmentation of the specimen or dressings.

**3. Culture.**
- On agar, medium-sized, mostly S colonies in 24 h at 37 °C. **At 48–72 h some cultures of *S. marcescens*, *S. rubidaea* and *S. plymuthica* produce a bright red pigment, prodigiosin, which is unmistakable.**
- On blood agar *S. marcescens* may give hemolysis over a **restricted** area.
- On selective media: **lactose-negative colonies that stay that way even after several days**.
- On egg yolk media: **lecithinase-positive** colonies.

**4a.** Motile Gram-negative rods.
**4b.** Medium S colonies, lactose-negative on selective media, red pigment at 48–72 h, restricted hemolysis, lecithinase +.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- TSI: glucose + (gas absent or minimal), lactose/sucrose – (late +), H₂S –.
- MILF: motile +, indole –, LDC +, PAD –.
- VP +, **DNase +, gelatinase +**, ONPG +.

**MAYA:** And there's an exam trap with *Salmonella* Typhi.

**ALEX:** A classic one. On TSI/MILF, *Serratia* and ***S.* Typhi look nearly identical**. Both produce no gas or very little. H₂S is absent, although *S.* Typhi may show a black dot at the stab. Neither ferments lactose or sucrose. Both are motile and indole-negative, both are lysine decarboxylase-positive and PAD-negative. **You settle it with ornithine decarboxylase, DNase, gelatinase, VP, and fermentation of adonitol, sorbitol and L-arabinose.**

**MAYA:** Species differentiation, Buiuc 31-23.

**ALEX:**
- ***S. marcescens***: LDC 99, ODC 99, gas 55, adonitol 40, **L-arabinose 0**, D-sorbitol 99, malonate 3, red pigment 30.
- ***S. liquefaciens***: L-arabinose 98, sorbitol 95.
- ***S. rubidaea***: ODC 0, adonitol 99, arabinose 100, malonate 94, pigment 50.
- ***S. odorifera***: LDC/ODC 100, arabinose 100. Its characteristic feature is a **putrid smell**.
- ***S. plymuthica***: LDC/ODC 0, pigment 20.

Definitive identification uses extended sets. The essentials are **lysine and ornithine decarboxylases, gas from glucose, and fermentation of adonitol, sorbitol and L-arabinose**.

**4d. Antigens.** Serological typing by O and H agglutination (Ewing-Davis extended by Grimont, over 70 serovars). This is the **preferred** method and widely available.
**4e.** Not done routinely.
**4f.** PCR for resistance genes; DNase, lipase and protease production as phenotypic markers.
**4g. Phage typing.** Schemes were proposed independently by **Farmer et al. (1995)** and **Traub et al. (1991)**, but they never spread widely. Serotyping is preferred.
**4h.** Bacteriocin typing. **Resistotyping** has important limits in this genus, because *S. marcescens* acquires multiple resistance factors so easily. Resistance to aminoglycosides (gentamicin, amikacin) and some cephalosporins can be relevant for hospital-circulating resistotypes. Molecular typing analyzes resistance plasmids.
**4i.** MALDI-TOF; PFGE and WGS/cgMLST for nosocomial outbreaks, which are **frequent in neonatology**.

**5. Susceptibility.**
- **Natural resistance**: aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, first-generation cephalosporins, cefoxitin, cefuroxime (inducible AmpC), **colistin/polymyxins** and nitrofurantoin.
- Frequent acquired resistance to third-generation cephalosporins (AmpC derepression), ESBLs and carbapenemases (**SME**, KPC, NDM).

### Serology / Treatment
**ALEX:** No serology. Treat according to the antibiogram, and **don't use colistin**.

## PART D — *CITROBACTER*

### Taxonomy

**ALEX:** The genus has 12 species. Buiuc Table 31-20 differentiates the 8 medically important ones: ***C. freundii*, *C. diversus* (= *C. koseri*)**, *C. amalonaticus, C. farmeri, C. youngae, C. braakii, C. werkmanii, C. sedlakii*. The current name of *C. diversus* is ***Citrobacter koseri***.

### Habitat

**ALEX:** Human and animal intestinal microbiota, and the environment. In feces **it can be confused with *Salmonella***.

### General characters

**ALEX:** **Motile** enterobacteria that ferment glucose **with gas**, lactose (**slowly**), dulcitol and cellobiose. They consistently produce **β-galactosidase** and grow on **KCN** media and on **Simmons citrate** as the sole carbon source. They **don't produce lysine decarboxylase or PAD**. Some species are urease-positive.

### Antigenic structure

**ALEX:**
- *C. freundii* has an antigenic structure similar to other motile enterobacteria: **40 O factors and 90 H factors**, defining **107 serovars**.
- **Serogroups O5 and O29 carry a Vi antigen that resembles, but isn't identical to, the Vi of *S.* Typhi.** That's a major source of diagnostic confusion.
- *C. diversus* has a scheme of 14 O factors and 7 H factors.

### Virulence factors

**ALEX:** Conditionally pathogenic. Enteric strains have intense **cytotoxic activity**. Endotoxin. ***C. koseri* has a particular tropism for the CNS in newborns**: it survives inside macrophages and forms **brain abscesses**.

### Clinical significance

**ALEX:**
- Most species have been reported in human disease, especially in immunosuppressed people.
- ***C. freundii***: accidental septicemia (post-transfusion), or spread from very varied local sites. It's also an **intestinal pathogen** and has been involved in **food-poisoning outbreaks**. Isolated from feces it **can be confused with *Salmonella enterica***, because they share biochemical and especially antigenic traits. It **can agglutinate with the polyvalent "O" and Vi anti-*Salmonella* sera**.
- ***C. diversus (koseri)***: septicemia, **neonatal meningitis**, UTIs, and less often enterocolitis. It's hard to distinguish from *E. coli*, especially in urinary isolates.
- *C. amalonaticus, C. farmeri, C. youngae*: sporadic isolates from nosocomial urinary and bronchopulmonary infections.

### Direct laboratory diagnosis

**1.** Feces, urine, blood, CSF (neonatology), bronchial aspirate.
**2.** Nonspecific; Gram-negative rods.
**3. Culture.** Culture behavior is shared with *Salmonella* and *Proteus*: it **behaves as a lactose-negative enterobacterium on selective media**. **On XLD it forms yellow colonies resembling *Proteus*.**
**4a.** Motile Gram-negative rods.
**4b.** S colonies, lactose-negative on selective media (slow lactose fermentation); yellow on XLD.

**4c. Biochemistry.**
- TSI + MILF separate the genus satisfactorily among lactose-negative groups. **Colonies that ferment glucose with gas, produce H₂S or indole, lack LDC and PAD, and are motile very probably belong to *Citrobacter*.**
- An 8-test set characterizes the species (Buiuc 31-20, % positive):
  - ***C. freundii***: indole 33, **H₂S 78**, **ODC 0**, malonate 11, sucrose 89, adonitol 0, dulcitol 11, raffinose 44;
  - ***C. diversus***: **indole 99**, **H₂S 0**, **ODC 99**, malonate 95, sucrose 40, adonitol 99, dulcitol 40, raffinose 0;
  - ***C. amalonaticus***: indole 100, ODC 95, malonate 1;
  - ***C. werkmanii***: indole 0, H₂S 100, malonate 100.
- **Indole, H₂S and ODC settle the distinction between *C. freundii* and *C. diversus*,** the two species of major medical importance.
- Definitive identification with extended tests can be difficult when separating from ***Escherichia*** (*C. amalonaticus, C. farmeri, C. diversus*) or from ***Enterobacter*** (*C. sedlakii*, H₂S-negative *C. freundii*).

**4d.** Serotyping only in specialized centres. **Watch for cross-reactions with anti-*Salmonella* sera (O and Vi).**
**4e.** Not routine; cytotoxicity of enteric strains can be tested on cell cultures.
**4f.** PCR for resistance genes (chromosomal AmpC **CMY**).
**4g.** Phage and bacteriocin typing schemes are used for *C. diversus*. ***C. amalonaticus* is neither sensitive to bacteriophages nor produces bacteriocins.**
**4h.** **Resistotyping** using antibiotics the genus is generally susceptible to: aminoglycosides, second- and third-generation cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones. The sporadically isolated species have no typing schemes.
**4i.** MALDI-TOF; WGS for neonatal outbreaks.

**5. Susceptibility.**
- ***C. freundii*** (and *C. braakii, C. youngae*): natural resistance to aminopenicillins, amoxicillin-clavulanate, first-generation cephalosporins and cefoxitin (**inducible AmpC**).
- ***C. koseri (diversus)***: natural resistance to aminopenicillins and ticarcillin (**chromosomal penicillinase**), **but susceptible to amoxicillin-clavulanate and first-generation cephalosporins**.

**MAYA:** So the two main *Citrobacter* species are opposites on amoxicillin-clavulanate.

**ALEX:** Exactly. *freundii* is an AmpC organism. *koseri* is a penicillinase organism, like *Klebsiella*.

### Serology / Treatment
**ALEX:** No serology. Treat according to the antibiogram. In **neonatal meningitis with *C. koseri***: **third-generation cephalosporin + aminoglycoside, with drainage of the abscesses**.

### Recap

**MAYA:** My four-genus summary:
- ***Klebsiella***: big capsule, non-motile, LDC +, urease +, VP +, citrate +, honey-drop mucoid colonies, redcurrant-jelly sputum. *K. oxytoca* is indole +. *K. ozaenae* is the only malonate-negative species. SHV-1 penicillinase means never ampicillin. KPC-Kp. String test for hvKp.
- ***Enterobacter***: motile, VP +, ODC +, LDC – except *aerogenes* and *gergoviae*. Inducible AmpC, so don't trust third-generation cephalosporins. *Cronobacter* in infant formula, 40–80% mortality. Yellow pigment in *sakazakii* and *agglomerans*.
- ***Serratia***: red prodigiosin at 48–72 h, DNase +, gelatinase +, VP +, late lactose. Mimics *S.* Typhi on TSI. Colistin-resistant. H antigen prepared with phenol 6‰.
- ***Citrobacter***: H₂S + or indole +, LDC –, citrate +, KCN +. Yellow on XLD. Agglutinates with anti-*Salmonella* O and Vi sera. *freundii* = AmpC, *koseri* = brain abscesses in neonates.
